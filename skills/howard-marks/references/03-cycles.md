# Market Cycles, Credit Cycles, and the Pendulum

> When to read this file: when analyzing where we stand in the market cycle, when evaluating credit conditions, when assessing whether market sentiment has reached an extreme, or when trying to understand why cycles repeat and how to position for them.

Marks considers cycle awareness the single most actionable element of his investment philosophy. His 2018 book "Mastering the Market Cycle" is entirely devoted to this topic.

> "You can't predict. You can prepare." — Mastering the Market Cycle

---

## The Nature of Cycles

### Why Cycles Happen

> "The basic reason for the cyclicality in our world is the involvement of humans. Mechanical things can go in a straight line. Humans can't." — Mastering the Market Cycle

Cycles exist because of human psychology:
1. Good news creates optimism → optimism creates risky behavior → risky behavior creates excess → excess creates problems
2. Problems create pessimism → pessimism creates cautious behavior → cautious behavior creates bargains → bargains create good news
3. The cycle repeats — endlessly, but never identically

> "The longer I've been investing, the more impressed I am with the power of the credit cycle. The economy rises and falls to a modest extent, but investor attitudes — and thus the availability of capital — tend to go from a point of great generosity to one of extreme stinginess." — Oaktree Memo

### The Three Key Cycles

**1. The Economic Cycle**
GDP growth oscillates around a long-term trend. Expansions last longer than contractions but always end. The economic cycle provides the fundamental backdrop.

**2. The Credit Cycle**
The most powerful and most dangerous cycle. When credit is abundant, bad deals get funded, standards deteriorate, and bubbles form. When credit contracts, even good businesses struggle to refinance.

> "The credit cycle contributes most to financial crises and has the biggest impact on the wellbeing of the financial markets." — Mastering the Market Cycle

**3. The Sentiment/Market Cycle**
Investor emotions amplify the economic and credit cycles. At tops, greed and complacency dominate. At bottoms, fear and despair dominate. Both extremes create opportunities.

## The Credit Cycle: Marks's Special Expertise

### The Virtuous and Vicious Circle

**The upswing (virtuous circle):**
1. Economy improves → corporate profits rise
2. Lenders become more willing to lend → credit is abundant and cheap
3. Companies borrow more → use leverage to grow or buy back stock
4. Rising prices and easy credit create confidence
5. Credit standards loosen ("covenant-lite" loans, PIK toggles, low documentation)
6. More and more marginal borrowers get funded
7. **This continues until something breaks**

**The downswing (vicious circle):**
1. Something breaks (a big default, a sector collapse, a macro shock)
2. Lenders panic → credit tightens suddenly
3. Companies can't refinance → defaults rise
4. Rising defaults scare lenders more → credit tightens further
5. Even healthy companies can't borrow → economic activity contracts
6. Prices collapse, creating distressed opportunities
7. **This continues until credit loosens again**

> "The credit cycle involves things that are literally binary: either capital is available to a borrower or it isn't. The credit window is either open or shut." — Mastering the Market Cycle

### Credit Cycle Indicators

| Indicator | Expansionary Signal | Late-Cycle Warning | Contractionary Signal |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| Credit spreads | Compressing toward historical lows | At or near all-time lows | Widening rapidly |
| Covenant quality | Standard covenants | "Covenant-lite" is normal | Investors demand strong covenants |
| Leverage multiples | Moderate (3-4x EBITDA) | High (6-7x+ EBITDA) | Falling (lenders won't fund high leverage) |
| Deal volume | Growing steadily | Frenzied, record-breaking | Collapsed |
| Default rates | Low and falling | Very low (the calm before) | Rising rapidly |
| New issuance quality | Mostly investment-grade | Heavy high-yield, CCC issuance | Markets closed for marginal issuers |

## The Pendulum of Investor Sentiment

> "The mood swings of the securities markets resemble the movement of a pendulum. Although the midpoint of its arc best describes the location of the pendulum 'on average,' it actually spends very little of its time there. Instead, it is almost always swinging toward or away from the extremes of the arc." — The Most Important Thing

### The Pendulum Never Stops in the Middle

Investor sentiment oscillates between two extremes:

**Extreme Greed (Top of Cycle):**
- "This time is different"
- "Prices can only go up"
- "Risk is low" (everyone's favorite dangerous phrase)
- FOMO drives buying
- Leverage increases
- Skeptics are ridiculed

**Extreme Fear (Bottom of Cycle):**
- "It's never going to recover"
- "This is the end"
- "Risk is everywhere" (when risk is actually lowest)
- Panic drives selling
- Leverage is forced to decrease
- Optimists are ridiculed

> "The pendulum metaphor is important because it reminds us that most great extremes are corrected by a swing to the opposite extreme, not by a swing to the midpoint." — Mastering the Market Cycle

### Marks's Pendulum Assessment Questions

Ask these at every market environment:

1. **Are investors optimistic or pessimistic?** (Listen to cocktail parties, not just analysts)
2. **Are the media talking about how great things are, or how terrible?**
3. **Are new investment vehicles being created to meet demand (IPOs, SPACs)?** → Usually a top signal
4. **Is credit readily available, even to weak borrowers?** → Late-cycle warning
5. **Are people bragging about their returns?** → Euphoria signal
6. **Are people afraid to invest at all?** → Fear signal — potential opportunity

## "Where Are We?" — The Most Important Question

> "We may never know where we're going, but we'd better have a good idea where we are." — Mastering the Market Cycle

### Marks's Cycle Positioning Framework

At any given time, assess where we stand on three dimensions:

**Dimension 1: Valuation Level**
| Level | Indicator | Action |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Cheap | P/E below 25th percentile of historical range | Increase risk / deploy capital |
| Fair | P/E near historical median | Normal positioning |
| Rich | P/E above 75th percentile | Reduce risk / build cash |
| Extreme | P/E above 90th percentile | Maximum defense |

**Dimension 2: Investor Behavior**
| Behavior | Indicator | Action |
|----------|-----------|--------|
| Fearful | Wide spreads, net outflows, low leverage | Increase risk — this is when opportunities are created |
| Cautious | Normal spreads, moderate flows | Normal positioning |
| Complacent | Tight spreads, net inflows, rising leverage | Reduce risk — this is when risk is building |
| Euphoric | Record-tight spreads, massive inflows, covenant-lite dominant | Maximum defense — crisis is coming |

**Dimension 3: Capital Availability**
| Availability | Indicator | Action |
|--------------|-----------|--------|
| Tight | Banks restricting lending, credit markets closed | Opportunity — good assets can be bought cheap from forced sellers |
| Normal | Standard lending standards, reasonable terms | Normal positioning |
| Loose | Easy terms, record issuance, low spreads | Caution — excess is building |
| Reckless | Anyone can borrow anything on any terms | Maximum defense — the peak is near |

### The Cycle Position Matrix

Combine the three dimensions to determine overall positioning:

| Combination | Positioning | Action |
|-------------|------------|--------|
| Cheap + Fearful + Tight | **Maximum Offense** | Deploy all available capital aggressively |
| Fair + Cautious + Normal | **Neutral** | Normal portfolio, selective opportunities |
| Rich + Complacent + Loose | **Defensive** | Reduce risk, build cash, increase quality |
| Extreme + Euphoric + Reckless | **Maximum Defense** | Maximum cash, maximum quality, prepare for crash |

> "The single most reliable indicator I know for where we stand in the cycle is the attitude of investors toward risk." — Oaktree Memo
