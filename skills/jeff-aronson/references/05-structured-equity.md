## The Structured Equity Universe

Between pure debt (with fixed contractual returns and no control) and pure equity (residual return with full exposure) lies a rich universe of **structured equity instruments** that combine elements of both: contractual or priority returns, downside protection features, and equity-like upside with governance rights.

Structured equity is not one instrument — it's a family of instruments configured to match a specific company's needs and an investor's risk/return objectives:

| Instrument | Priority | Return Type | Governance | Typical Use Case |
|------------|----------|-------------|------------|-----------------|
| Senior Preferred Equity | Above common | Fixed dividend + liquidation preference | Strong (board seats, vetoes) | Rescue financing for distressed but solvent companies |
| Convertible Preferred | Above common | Fixed dividend + conversion option | Moderate (anti-dilution, board seat) | Growth companies needing flexible capital |
| PIK Toggle Notes | Debt senior | PIK or cash interest at company's election | Moderate (covenants) | LBOs with tight cash flow; bridge financing |
| Convertible Notes | Debt senior | Fixed coupon + conversion option | Light (covenants + conversion) | Bridge to equity; venture-stage companies |
| Warrants / Equity Kickers | Subordinated | Upside participation only | None | Added to debt to sweeten returns |
| Participating Preferred | Above common | Fixed dividend + equity participation | Strong | Venture-style protection in growth equity |

## Preferred Equity: The Core Centerbridge Instrument

### What Is Preferred Equity?

Preferred equity sits above common equity in the capital structure waterfall but below debt. Key features:

**Liquidation Preference**: In a liquidation or sale, preferred holders receive their invested capital (and any accrued unpaid dividends) before common equity holders receive anything.

**Dividends**: Preferred equity typically carries a fixed dividend, which may be:
- **Cash pay**: Must be paid currently in cash; increases cash burn
- **PIK (Payment in Kind)**: Accrues as additional preferred shares or as an increase to the liquidation preference; company doesn't need cash but the liquidation preference compounds
- **Toggle**: Company can elect each period whether to pay cash or PIK

**Conversion**: Most preferred equity includes conversion rights — the preferred holder can convert their preferred shares into common equity at a predetermined ratio. This is the equity upside feature.

**Redemption**: Preferred equity typically carries a mandatory or optional redemption provision — the company can (or must) repurchase the preferred at liquidation preference + accrued after a set period.

### Why Preferred Equity Works for Both Parties

**For the company (family business, management team, carve-out)**:
- Does not require fixed cash interest payments (PIK preserves cash)
- Does not dilute common equity immediately (unlike issuing new common shares)
- Preserves management and founder control (preferred holder doesn't necessarily own common shares)
- Faster to execute than a bank loan (no lengthy credit approval process)
- More flexible covenants than bank debt

**For the investor (Centerbridge)**:
- Priority over common equity (protected in downside scenarios)
- Fixed return on invested capital (liquidation preference + accrued dividend)
- Governance rights without control (see below)
- Conversion option provides equity upside if the company recovers strongly
- Not subject to covenant trip points that trigger debt defaults

### Governance Rights Without Control

The centerpiece of Centerbridge's preferred equity strategy is obtaining meaningful governance rights **without** requiring a controlling equity stake. This is important for:

- Family businesses that will not sell control
- Management teams doing carve-outs who want to retain ownership
- Companies that cannot use debt but do not want a PE buyout

**Typical governance package in Centerbridge preferred equity**:

1. **Board Representation**: One or two board seats (or observer rights). Provides visibility into strategy and operations; creates fiduciary duty channel for information.

2. **Veto Rights on Major Decisions**: Preferred holder must approve:
   - Additional debt above a threshold (e.g., cannot incur >$100M of new debt without consent)
   - Acquisitions above a threshold
   - Sale of material assets
   - Changes to the business plan / strategy
   - Additional equity issuances that dilute preferred
   - Any equity distribution to common holders before preferred is redeemed

3. **Protective Provisions**: Automatic triggers if the company deteriorates:
   - Preferred dividend steps up if not redeemed by Year 3
   - Additional board seats if certain performance metrics are missed
   - Redemption becomes mandatory if leverage exceeds a threshold

4. **Information Rights**: Full financial reporting, management calls, budget reviews — all the access of a board member without necessarily having a board seat.

> "We often describe our preferred equity as 'governance without control.' We don't run the company. But we have enough influence to protect our investment and enough upside to participate if it goes well."

## PIK Instruments: Payment in Kind

### What Is PIK?

PIK (Payment in Kind) instruments do not require cash interest payments. Instead, interest accrues as additional principal (on PIK debt) or as additional preferred shares or liquidation preference (on PIK preferred equity).

**Example**: A $100M PIK note at 10% per year:
- Year 1: No cash payment; new principal = $110M
- Year 2: No cash payment; new principal = $121M
- Year 3: No cash payment; new principal = $133M
- At maturity: Must repay $133M in cash (or the note converts to equity)

**Why PIK is useful**:
- Preserves cash during the period when the company is building FCF or paying down senior debt
- Common in LBOs where cash goes first to repay senior secured debt
- Allows highly leveraged structures to function without immediate cash interest burden
- Provides runway for an operational recovery before the capital structure must be addressed

### PIK Toggle Notes

PIK toggle notes give the company an **election** each payment period to pay either:
- Cash interest at the stated rate (e.g., 8%)
- PIK interest at a slightly higher rate (e.g., 8.5% — the higher rate compensates lenders for the PIK election)

**When companies toggle to PIK**:
Companies typically elect PIK when they are under cash flow pressure. The market watches toggle elections closely — a company switching from cash to PIK is a significant stress signal, often causing bond spreads to widen sharply.

**For the investor**: PIK instruments are most attractive in the middle of the capital structure — junior enough to require the PIK feature (senior debt gets cash), but senior enough that the accruing balance is genuinely recoverable. Deeply subordinated PIK instruments (holdco PIK) are often worth near zero in distressed scenarios because the accruing balance grows faster than the company can generate value to cover it.

## Convertible Structures

### Convertible Bonds

A convertible bond pays a fixed coupon (typically below-market, because the conversion option has value) and gives the holder the right to convert into common equity at a predetermined conversion price.

**Conversion premium**: The conversion price is typically set at 20–35% above the stock price at issuance. If the stock price rises above the conversion price, the bond is "in the money" and the holder may convert to equity.

**Why convertibles work in the Centerbridge context**:
- Provides debt-like downside protection (the bond has a floor in value — the credit value of the fixed income portion)
- Provides equity-like upside (if the business recovers, the equity conversion option becomes valuable)
- Typically less dilutive to existing common shareholders than issuing new common equity
- Lower current coupon than straight debt preserves cash

### Convertible Preferred in Restructuring

When Centerbridge provides rescue financing as convertible preferred:
- **Structure**: Preferred equity with a fixed PIK dividend (e.g., 12% PIK) and conversion rights at an agreed valuation
- **Conversion trigger**: Company can force conversion (or investor can elect) after a liquidity event (IPO, sale) or after a defined period
- **Downside**: Liquidation preference protects the invested capital + accrued PIK
- **Upside**: If company executes well and is sold at 3x the valuation implied by the conversion price, the investor converts to common and participates in the 3x appreciation

## Structured Equity for Specific Situations

### Family Business / Founder Situation

A founder-controlled company needs capital for growth or a liquidity event (e.g., buying out a retiring co-founder) but does not want to sell control or take on restrictive bank debt.

**Centerbridge solution**: Minority preferred equity position with:
- PIK dividend that accrues but doesn't require cash
- Mandatory redemption at 3× the investment after 5 years (from an eventual sale or refinancing)
- Board observer seat (not a full board seat, preserving founder control)
- Veto on additional debt and material asset sales
- Conversion option into 15–20% of common equity if the company is sold above a threshold valuation

**Why this works**: The founder keeps control, the company gets the capital it needs without bank oversight, and Centerbridge gets downside protection (liquidation preference + mandatory redemption) plus equity upside.

### Corporate Carve-Out

A large corporation is selling a non-core division. The management team of the division wants to buy it (management buyout) but cannot raise enough bank debt, and private equity sponsors demand too much equity control.

**Centerbridge solution**: Provide structured equity as part of the MBO:
- First lien bank debt (or Centerbridge provides the first lien itself)
- Centerbridge structured equity: convertible preferred with governance rights
- Management equity: common shares that management buys (small stake keeps management motivated without PE control)

**Why this works**: The carve-out gets done without PE control. Management retains meaningful equity. Centerbridge has governance protection and equity upside. Banks get their normal first lien position.

### Distressed Company Rescue

A company is stressed (not yet defaulted) and needs capital to avoid default, but traditional lenders won't extend new credit.

**Centerbridge solution**: Rescue preferred equity injected at the holdco level:
- Provides cash to repay upcoming bank maturities
- Ranks senior to common equity (protects Centerbridge in a subsequent restructuring)
- Carries higher PIK dividend that accrues (8–12% PIK)
- Comes with extensive governance rights (board seat + vetoes)
- Includes conversion option into a large block of common equity at a depressed valuation

**Key insight**: By coming in above equity but below existing debt, Centerbridge creates a position that is protected in downside scenarios (existing equity gets wiped out before the preferred is impaired) and participates in upside scenarios (conversion option). The existing senior debt holders don't mind because Centerbridge's capital reduces their refinancing risk.

## Governance Without Control: The Philosophical Underpinning

The structured equity approach reflects a fundamental Centerbridge philosophy: **you do not need to control a company to protect your investment.** Traditional PE firms require control because their model is operational improvement — they need the authority to fire management, restructure operations, and sell the company. Centerbridge's model is different:

1. **Identify a fundamentally sound business with a capital structure problem**
2. **Provide the capital solution that solves the problem**
3. **Secure governance rights that protect the investment without requiring operational control**
4. **Wait for the business to recover and generate the promised return**

This means Centerbridge can invest in situations that PE firms cannot: family businesses that won't sell control, management teams that won't accept PE oversight, companies that need a limited partner rather than a controlling sponsor.

> "Our job is to solve the problem and get paid for solving it. We're not in the business of running companies. We're in the business of finding companies with problems we can solve — and being the only one with the right instrument to solve them."

## Key Structured Equity Metrics

| Metric | Calculation | Benchmark |
|--------|------------|-----------|
| PIK Effective Return | Starting dividend rate + compounding of accrued PIK | Target: 12–18% IRR |
| Liquidation Coverage | (Enterprise Value − Senior Debt) ÷ Preferred Liquidation Preference | Target: >1.5x |
| Conversion Upside | (Current EV × Target Multiple − Conversion Price) ÷ Investment | Target: 2–3x equity upside |
| Time to Redemption | Negotiated; typically 3–5 years | Shorter = less interest rate risk |
| Governance Veto Coverage | % of major decisions subject to veto | Target: all debt + M&A + dividends |
