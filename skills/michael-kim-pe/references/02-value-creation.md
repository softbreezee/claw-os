# Value Creation: Operational Improvement Playbook

> "The difference between PE and passive investing is that we do not wait for value to be recognized. We create it."

PE's operational playbook is not about cutting costs to the bone. It is about making businesses structurally better — more defensible, more cash-generative, more scalable — so that at exit, multiple buyers want what you've built.

---

## 1. Revenue Growth Levers

Revenue growth is the highest-quality source of PE returns because it flows through to EBITDA and exit multiple simultaneously.

### Lever 1A: Pricing Optimization

**Thesis:** Most founder-run businesses systematically underprice. Founders fear losing customers; PE owners test the market.

**Execution steps:**
1. Map customer cohorts by revenue, margin, and tenure
2. Identify the 20% of customers with lowest price sensitivity (strategic/sticky accounts)
3. Test price increases 5–10% on renewal cycles with low-risk cohorts
4. Measure churn; if < 3%, expand pricing action to next cohort
5. Introduce value-based pricing for premium tiers or new features

**EBITDA impact:** 3–5% revenue price increase on 60% flow-through = 1.8–3.0 EBITDA margin points

**Asian context:** Korean and Japanese business cultures have strong resistance to price increases; frame as "value alignment" not "price increase." Build customer intimacy before raising.

### Lever 1B: Cross-Sell and Upsell

**Thesis:** Existing customers have already crossed the trust barrier; selling more to them has near-zero CAC.

**Execution steps:**
1. Map the product/service adjacencies (what do customers buy elsewhere?)
2. Analyze purchase patterns: who buys Product A but not Product B?
3. Create bundled offerings with internal sales incentives
4. Assign dedicated key account managers to top 20 customers
5. Track attach rate and revenue per customer as monthly KPIs

**EBITDA impact:** 5–15% revenue uplift from cross-sell at very high margins (near 100% incremental contribution)

### Lever 1C: Geographic Expansion

**Thesis:** A proven business model in one market can often be replicated in adjacent markets with lower capital intensity than greenfield.

**When to execute:**
- Core market share > 20% (proven playbook)
- Target market has similar regulatory and customer profile
- Existing management has bandwidth or hire is ready

**MBK-specific:** Korean-to-Japan (culturally proximate, aging demographics), Japan-to-Korea reverse, China domestic geographic expansion (tier-1 to tier-2 cities)

**EBITDA impact:** Variable; typically 3-5 years to profitability per new market. Model conservatively (negative EBITDA years 1-2).

## 2. Margin Expansion Tactics

Margin expansion that is structural (not one-time cost cuts) is what creates permanent value.

### Lever 2A: Cost Reduction — Labor and Overhead

**Sustainable cost reduction (structural):**
- Consolidate overlapping functions post-acquisition (finance, HR, IT, legal)
- Relocate back-office to lower-cost locations (Korean firms: move support to regional offices)
- Eliminate management layers (de-layer: remove one level between CEO and front line)
- Automate repetitive processes (AP/AR, payroll, order entry)

**Unsustainable cost reduction (avoid):**
- Cutting R&D below maintenance level
- Reducing customer-facing headcount below service quality floor
- Deferring essential capex (borrowed-time savings)

**Typical savings:** 1–3% of revenue from structural overhead rationalization

### Lever 2B: Procurement and Vendor Consolidation

**Thesis:** Founder-run businesses rarely have professional procurement. PE brings scale and discipline.

**Execution steps:**
1. Audit all vendor contracts (top 50 vendors by spend)
2. Identify overlap and opportunities for consolidation
3. Run competitive RFPs for the top 10 spend categories
4. Negotiate multi-year framework agreements with volume commitments
5. Centralize procurement under a CPO or Finance Controller

**Savings benchmark:** 5–15% on addressable spend; addressable is typically 30–50% of COGS

**EBITDA impact:** 1–3 margin points for procurement-intensive businesses (manufacturing, distribution)

### Lever 2C: Shared Services and Platform Model

**Thesis:** In a portfolio with multiple companies in similar sectors, shared services create cost leverage.

**Services to centralize:**
- Finance and accounting (shared CFO or outsourced)
- HR and benefits administration (consolidated benefits buying power)
- IT infrastructure (shared ERP, cybersecurity, cloud)
- Legal (preferred outside counsel at discounted rates)

**Applicable to:** Buy-and-build strategies where multiple add-ons share back-office

## 3. Working Capital Optimization

Working capital is trapped cash. Releasing it creates immediate equity value and improves FCF conversion.

### Key Working Capital Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Target Improvement |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) | A/R / (Revenue/365) | Reduce by 5–10 days |
| Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) | Inventory / (COGS/365) | Reduce by 10–20 days |
| Days Payable Outstanding (DPO) | A/P / (COGS/365) | Extend by 5–15 days |
| Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) | DSO + DIO − DPO | Target CCC < 30 days |

**Cash released formula:**
```
Cash released = (Days improvement / 365) × Revenue
Example: 10-day DSO reduction on $500M revenue = $13.7M one-time cash release
```

### Execution Approach

**DSO reduction:**
- Implement early payment discounts (2/10 net 30)
- Hire dedicated collections team for overdue accounts
- Require upfront deposits on large contracts
- Invoice immediately at delivery (billing cycle discipline)

**DIO reduction:**
- SKU rationalization (eliminate slow-moving inventory)
- Demand-driven ordering (reduce safety stock with better forecasting)
- Consignment arrangements with major suppliers

**DPO extension:**
- Negotiate payment terms from 30 → 45 or 60 days with suppliers
- Use supply chain finance programs (reverse factoring)
- Standardize payment terms across the entity

## 4. Add-On Acquisitions (Buy-and-Build Strategy)

Buy-and-build is PE's most powerful value creation lever: buy a platform at X multiple, add bolt-ons at X−2 to X−4 multiple, and exit the combined entity at X+ multiple.

### Why Buy-and-Build Works

```
Platform acquisition:   $100M EBITDA @ 10x = $1,000M EV
Add-on 1:               $20M EBITDA @ 7x  = $140M EV  (paid)
Add-on 2:               $15M EBITDA @ 6x  = $90M EV   (paid)
Add-on 3:               $10M EBITDA @ 6x  = $60M EV   (paid)

Combined EBITDA:        $145M
Exit at 10x (platform): $1,450M EV
Add-on cost basis:      $290M

Multiple arbitrage gain: ($145M − $100M) × (10x − 6.5x avg) = $157M extra equity
```

### Add-On Screening Criteria

- **Strategic fit:** Same customer base, adjacent product, overlapping distribution
- **Integration simplicity:** Small enough to integrate without disrupting platform
- **Financial threshold:** EBITDA positive; no turnarounds as add-ons
- **Geographic:** Same or adjacent market (avoid cross-border complexity for small add-ons)
- **Price discipline:** Pay < platform multiple; synergies must be real and quantified

### Integration Playbook

**Week 1–2:** Announce, retain key people, communicate clearly
**Week 3–8:** Finance integration (single GL, reporting under platform CFO)
**Month 2–3:** Procurement consolidation; shared services transfer
**Month 3–6:** Sales team alignment; cross-sell program launch
**Month 6–12:** Full system migration (ERP, CRM, HR); track synergy realization vs. plan

## 5. The 100-Day Plan Framework

The 100-day plan is the tactical execution document for the first phase of PE ownership. It converts the investment thesis into specific, accountable actions.

### Structure

**Pre-Close Preparation (Days −30 to 0):**
- Finalize management team; identify gaps
- Appoint board members; set governance cadence (monthly board meetings minimum)
- Design reporting package (weekly flash, monthly P&L, quarterly strategic review)
- Draft 100-day priority list with CEO; get alignment before close

**Days 1–30: Foundation**
- [ ] Implement weekly management reporting to PE owner
- [ ] Complete financial close within 15 days of month-end (vs. typical 30–45 for founder-run)
- [ ] Meet top 20 customers personally (CEO + PE representative)
- [ ] Complete organization chart; identify roles to fill / rationalize
- [ ] Begin cost audit: identify first-90-day quick wins

**Days 31–60: Quick Wins**
- [ ] Execute pricing adjustment on most defensible customer cohort
- [ ] Launch procurement RFP for top 3 spend categories
- [ ] DSO reduction: implement new invoicing and collections protocol
- [ ] First add-on target identified and initial outreach made
- [ ] Technology audit completed; system migration plan drafted

**Days 61–100: Structural Improvements**
- [ ] Q1 board presentation with Year 1 budget vs. LBO model
- [ ] Operating partner embedded (if applicable)
- [ ] HR: compensation redesign including management equity plan
- [ ] At least one specific EBITDA improvement initiative completed with $ impact tracked
- [ ] 3-year strategic plan drafted with specific milestones

## Value Creation: Prioritization Matrix

When faced with 20 possible initiatives, rank by:

| Initiative | EBITDA Impact ($M) | Speed to Realize | Capital Required | Risk | Priority |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|-----------------|------|---------|
| Pricing +5% | $3–6M | < 6 months | Low | Low | **1st** |
| Procurement RFP | $2–5M | 3–9 months | Low | Low | **1st** |
| DSO reduction | Cash, not EBITDA | 1–3 months | None | Low | **1st** |
| Headcount rationalization | $1–4M | 1–3 months | Low | Medium | **2nd** |
| Geographic expansion | $5–20M | 2–4 years | Medium-High | High | **3rd** |
| New product launch | $3–15M | 1–3 years | Medium | High | **3rd** |
| Add-on acquisition | $5–30M | 1–3 years | High | High | **2nd** (if pipeline ready) |

**Rule:** Do the fast, cheap, low-risk items first. Use early wins to build credibility and fund riskier initiatives.
