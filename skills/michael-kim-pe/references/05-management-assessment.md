# Management Assessment: PE-Style Evaluation

> "We are not hiring a manager. We are selecting a partner who will execute our thesis with their team, under our governance, using our capital. The stakes of getting this wrong are enormous."

In PE, management evaluation is not an HR exercise. You are assessing whether a specific human being (and their team) can execute a specific value creation plan in a specific industry context under the pressure of PE ownership — typically for the first time in their career.

---

## 1. The PE Management Paradox

Founder-managers who built successful companies are often the wrong people to run a PE-backed business. The skills that built the company (entrepreneurial risk-taking, intuition-driven decisions, relationship-based management) are not the same skills required to run a PE-backed company (process discipline, financial rigor, monthly accountability, comfort with leverage, and orientation toward a defined exit).

**Pre-PE success ≠ PE success.**

| Skill | Family/Founder Company | PE-Backed Company |
|-------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Decision-making | Intuition + relationship | Data + process |
| Speed | Deliberate (owner's capital) | Fast (LBO clock is running) |
| Financial reporting | Quarterly at best; loose | Monthly flash; ≤ 15-day close |
| Board relationship | Founder IS the board | Board holds CEO accountable |
| Hiring | Relationship hires | Hire for capability, fire fast |
| Exit orientation | "I'll run this forever" | "I'm working toward a defined liquidity event" |

## 2. Can They Execute a 100-Day Plan?

The 100-day plan is the stress test of PE management readiness. Evaluate this specifically:

### Assessment Questions (ask in management interviews)

**Planning capability:**
- "Walk me through the three things you would focus on in the first 100 days if we closed today."
- Expected answer: Specific, sequenced, prioritized. Not vague platitudes.
- Red flag answer: "We need to evaluate the business first" (no conviction) or a laundry list of 20 things (no prioritization)

**Accountability culture:**
- "Tell me about a time you missed a target. What happened and what did you do?"
- Expected answer: Owns it, explains the root cause, describes the corrective action
- Red flag answer: External blame (market conditions, employees, etc.) with no self-accountability

**Execution speed:**
- "What is the fastest you have moved on a significant decision? What enabled that speed?"
- PE requires decisions in weeks that founder-run companies make over months

**Financial fluency:**
- "What is the current monthly run-rate EBITDA vs. plan? What is driving the variance?"
- A PE-ready CEO should know this number within 5%. If they don't, they are not managing by the numbers.

### 100-Day Plan Simulation (best practice)

Before signing the deal, ask management to present a draft 100-day plan. Evaluate:
- Is it specific or generic?
- Are the EBITDA impact estimates grounded in data?
- Does it reflect understanding of the LBO constraints (debt service, reporting requirements)?
- Is the team aligned (CFO presenting alongside CEO, not CEO explaining CFO's work)?

## 3. Operating Partner Fit

Most PE investments benefit from an operating partner — a senior executive with domain expertise who works alongside management to accelerate value creation.

### When to Add an Operating Partner

- Management team is strong operationally but weak financially (need operating CFO)
- Industry transformation underway (e.g., digital, AI) and management is not current
- Buy-and-build strategy requires M&A capability management doesn't have
- CEO is the founder who needs a "deputy for accountability" without losing face
- International expansion requires executive with target market expertise

### Operating Partner Profiles

**Interim CFO / Finance partner:**
- Needed when: CFO cannot close books in 15 days; PE reporting sophistication lacking
- Profile: Former CFO of PE-backed company; understands sponsor reporting, covenant compliance, board communication

**Revenue Growth partner:**
- Needed when: Sales organization is under-professionalized; pricing power untapped
- Profile: Former CCO or VP Sales at scale; has built compensation structures and sales enablement

**Operations / supply chain partner:**
- Needed when: Manufacturing or logistics efficiency is a core thesis driver
- Profile: Former COO of industrial company; lean manufacturing / operations excellence background

**Digital transformation partner:**
- Needed when: Technology investment is required and management is not digital-native
- Profile: Former CTO or Chief Digital Officer; has led ERP implementations, digital customer journey work

### Introducing an Operating Partner Without Breaking Management Trust

**The framing:** Present as a resource, not as oversight. "We want to give you the support infrastructure to achieve what we've outlined together."

**Reporting structure options:**
- Direct report to CEO (preferred; CEO owns the relationship)
- Direct report to PE board member (less preferred; creates parallel authority)
- Time-limited (12–18 months with defined handoff)

**Red flag:** Management resists operating partner without explanation. Suggests defensiveness or lack of confidence in their own plan.

## 4. Management Equity Rollover (Skin in the Game)

Equity rollover is the most important alignment mechanism in PE. If management does not roll equity, alignment is fundamentally broken.

### Standard Equity Rollover Framework

**How it works:**
- Management exchanges a portion of their deal proceeds for equity in the new PE-owned company
- Equity subject to a vesting schedule (typically 4-year ratable vest, with 1-year cliff)
- "Good leaver" vs. "bad leaver" provisions: leaving with cause = forfeiture

**Target rollover amounts:**

| Executive | Typical Rollover | Rationale |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| CEO | $5–30M+ (deal-dependent) | Must feel financial pain if things go wrong |
| CFO | $2–10M | Critical implementation partner |
| Division heads | $1–5M each | Operational accountability |

**Minimum acceptable:** CEO rollover should equal at least 2 years of total compensation. Below this level, the CEO is financially better off leaving if the business underperforms.

**Red flag — CEO refuses to roll:** This is a serious signal. Possible interpretations:
- CEO does not believe in the business
- CEO has information about risks not shared in DD
- CEO plans to leave shortly after close
- PE investor should walk away or dramatically reprice the deal

### Equity Incentive Plan Design

**Recommended structure:**
- **Base equity grant:** Granted at close; subject to time-based vesting
- **Performance equity:** Additional tranche unlocked at 2.5x MOIC (at-target) and 3.5x MOIC (maximum)
- **Total pool size:** 10–15% of exit equity for management (across all participants)

**Waterfall mechanics:**
```
Exit Equity distributed as:
  1. Return of PE capital (equity check)
  2. PE preferred return (typically 8% hurdle)
  3. Remaining equity split: 85% to PE / 15% to management pool
```

## 5. Reporting Capability Assessment

PE owners live and die by the quality of management information. Inadequate reporting is a value-creation killer — you cannot improve what you cannot measure.

### Minimum PE Reporting Standard

| Report | Frequency | Delivery Timing | What It Must Contain |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Flash report | Weekly | Monday morning | Revenue, orders, key operational KPIs |
| Management P&L | Monthly | 15th of following month (or better) | Full P&L with variance to budget |
| Cash flow | Monthly | 15th of following month | Actuals vs. forecast; liquidity runway |
| Board package | Quarterly | 2 weeks before meeting | P&L, balance sheet, CF, strategic update |
| Annual budget | Annually | November 30 (for January start) | Full 3-statement model with KPIs |

**Assessment questions:**
- "How long does it take you to close the books each month?" (Answer should be < 15 days)
- "What is your current financial system?" (Excel-heavy = red flag for $50M+ EBITDA businesses)
- "Show me last month's management accounts." (Evaluate format, timeliness, commentary quality)

**Red flags in reporting:**
- No chart of accounts aligned to management reporting
- P&L closes > 30 days after month-end
- No budget vs. actual variance analysis
- Revenue and cost centers not clearly defined

## 6. Replacement Bench Strength

PE investment horizons span 3–5 years. The CEO is one health crisis, personal decision, or performance miss away from needing replacement. The question is never "if" but "how prepared are we if this happens?"

### Bench Assessment

**Internal successors (preferred — faster, less disruptive):**
- Who is the #2 executive? Could they run the company on Day 1?
- What is the gap between the CEO and the #2?
- Has the #2 been exposed to PE-level reporting and governance?

**External succession (backup plan):**
- Have a shortlist of 3 external CEO candidates before the deal closes (not announced)
- Work with executive search firms to maintain a live pipeline
- Operating partners as bridge while permanent succession is executed

**Asian succession complexity:**
- In Korean companies, the CEO's authority is tied to relationships with banks, customers, and government. A replacement CEO needs 6–12 months to establish equivalent relationships.
- In Japanese companies, new leadership from outside is often not accepted quickly; promote from within is strongly preferred (and typically required for employee buy-in)
- China: Founding team often holds regulatory relationships that are non-transferable — loss of founders can have direct regulatory consequences

## 7. Cultural Fit with PE Ownership Model

The PE ownership model is fundamentally different from founder or public company ownership. Management must actively adapt.

### What PE Ownership Requires

- **Monthly accountability:** Every number reviewed, every variance explained
- **Board relationship:** Treating the board as a true supervisor, not an advisory committee
- **Speed:** Decisions made in weeks, not quarters
- **Communication:** Proactive communication of problems (bad news early, not hidden)
- **Exit orientation:** Building for sale; understanding that the goal is eventual liquidity

### Assessing Cultural Fit (Interview Signals)

**Green flags:**
- CEO has worked for a PE-backed company before
- CEO proactively asks about reporting requirements and board cadence
- CEO describes their management style as "data-driven" with specific examples
- CEO frames the partnership as "We're working toward the same exit together"

**Red flags:**
- CEO describes PE involvement as "they just give us money and stay out of our way"
- CEO asks to reduce board meeting frequency or monthly reporting
- CEO does not mention the LBO model or exit as something they think about
- CEO refers to the business as "my company" in ways that suggest they won't share control

### Asian Cultural Adaptation Nuances

**Korean executives:** Strong hierarchy orientation; will follow PE direction once authority is established, but require respect for face and seniority. Do not challenge the CEO's decisions publicly.

**Japanese executives:** Consensus-oriented; will resist rapid change even when intellectually aligned. Change management requires extensive internal communication and the "nemawashi" process — build internal support before any major initiative.

**Chinese executives:** High risk tolerance; fast decision-making; strong personal network dependence. Key risk: founder relationship risks departing with founder; network does not transfer.
