# Exit Strategies: Returning Capital to LPs

> "Buying a great business is art. Exiting at the right price, through the right channel, at the right time — that is the science that separates top-quartile PE from the rest."

In PE, everything that happens between acquisition and exit is just operations. The exit is the moment of truth — when all the value creation work is translated into realized returns for LPs. Exit strategy must be considered on Day 1 of ownership, not Year 4.

---

## 1. Exit Route Overview

| Exit Route | Best When | Typical Premium | Timeline | Complexity |
|------------|----------|----------------|----------|------------|
| **IPO** | Market is hot; company has public comps trading at premium multiples; growth story resonates with public investors | 0–20% premium to strategic at peak | 12–24 months prep; 6-month lock-up post-IPO | High |
| **Strategic Sale** | Synergy buyer exists; industry consolidation underway; management team attractive to acquirer | 20–40% synergy premium | 6–18 months process | Medium |
| **Secondary Buyout (SBO)** | Company not yet ready for IPO; another PE firm sees value creation runway; quick exit needed | At or slight discount to strategic | 4–8 months (buyer motivated) | Low-Medium |
| **Dividend Recap** | Not an exit but returns capital mid-hold; business has deleveraged and has borrowing capacity | N/A (capital return, not exit) | 1–3 months | Low |
| **Management Buyout (MBO)** | Management team has capital/backing; used for smaller assets or portfolio companies | Typically below market (management leverage) | 3–6 months | Low |

**MBK's preferred ranking (by returns historically):** Strategic Sale > IPO > Secondary Buyout

## 2. IPO Exit

### When PE Takes a Company Public

IPO is the highest-profile exit and can deliver the highest absolute valuation — but only under specific conditions:

**IPO readiness criteria:**
- [ ] EBITDA ≥ $100M (below this, public markets provide thin liquidity and poor coverage)
- [ ] Revenue growth ≥ 10% per year (public investors pay for growth)
- [ ] Clear market leadership position (#1 or strong #2 in defined market)
- [ ] 3 years of clean audited financials (GAAP/IFRS compliant)
- [ ] Financial reporting infrastructure capable of quarterly public reporting
- [ ] No ongoing material litigation
- [ ] Management team comfortable with public company scrutiny

**Asian IPO venues:**
| Market | Best For | Typical P/E or EV/EBITDA | Liquidity |
|--------|---------|--------------------------|-----------|
| KOSPI/KOSDAQ (Korea) | Korean domestic businesses; consumer, industrial, tech | 12–20x EV/EBITDA | Good for large-cap; thin for mid-cap |
| TSE Prime (Japan) | Japanese domestic; stable, cash-generative businesses | 10–16x EV/EBITDA | Deep; long-term holders |
| HKEX (Hong Kong) | China-exposed businesses; international investor access | Compressed post-2021; deal by deal | Recovering |
| NYSE/Nasdaq (US) | High-growth; global brand; tech-enabled | 15–25x EV/EBITDA for quality | Best in world |
| SGX (Singapore) | Southeast Asia exposure; smaller mid-cap | 10–14x EV/EBITDA | Thin |

### IPO Process Timeline

```
T−18 months: IPO readiness assessment; audit firm upgrade if needed
T−12 months: Appoint investment banks (bookrunners + co-managers); pre-IPO roadshow
T−9 months:  File draft registration statement (F-1 / prospectus)
T−6 months:  SEC/regulator comment process; respond to queries
T−3 months:  Investor education; pre-deal roadshow; pricing discussions
T−0:         Price and list; PE typically retains 60–80% of shares
T+6 months:  Lock-up expiry; PE begins secondary sales

Total PE exit: 12–24 months to full sell-down after IPO
```

### IPO Lock-Up Management

PE cannot sell at the IPO — all shares are locked up for 6 months (standard). Post-lock-up:
- Sell in secondary offerings (registered blocks)
- Sell via accelerated book builds (ABB) — sell in one trading session, minimize market impact
- Sell into the public market (10b5-1 plan) over extended period
- Distribute shares in-kind to LPs (in-kind distribution — rare, LP preference varies)

**Risk:** Market window closes. If the public market deteriorates post-IPO, the PE firm is trapped with an illiquid position that has lost value. IPO as exit route requires healthy public markets — a macro variable beyond PE control.

## 3. Strategic Sale

### The Strategic Premium

Strategic buyers pay a premium because they value the acquisition through the lens of synergies — cost savings and revenue opportunities they can achieve that a financial buyer cannot.

**Synergy types:**
| Synergy | Example | Typical Value |
|---------|---------|--------------|
| Cost synergies | Eliminate duplicate corporate functions | 3–8% of combined revenue |
| Revenue synergies | Cross-sell to combined customer base | 2–5% incremental revenue |
| Geographic | Expand target into acquirer's markets | Deal-specific |
| Technology | Incorporate target's tech into acquirer's platform | Deal-specific |

**Synergy premium formula:**
```
Strategic EV = Financial Buyer EV + NPV of Synergies × [sharing ratio]

Example:
  Financial buyer EV:   $1,000M (10x × $100M EBITDA)
  Total synergies:      $30M/year → $200M NPV at 15% discount rate
  Sharing ratio:        50% (strategic keeps half, target gets half)
  Strategic premium:    $100M
  Strategic EV:         $1,100M (11x effective multiple)
```

### Strategic Sale Process

**Option 1: Proprietary / bilateral negotiation**
- Best for: Known interested strategic buyer; avoid competitive auction
- Advantage: Speed, confidentiality, relationship preservation
- Disadvantage: No competitive tension; may leave money on the table

**Option 2: Controlled auction (preferred for maximizing price)**
- Phase 1: Identify 6–10 potential buyers (strategic + 2–3 large PE firms)
- Phase 2: Send teaser; receive NDAs; send CIM (Confidential Information Memorandum)
- Phase 3: Non-binding indicative bids; short-list to 3–4 parties
- Phase 4: Management presentations; final bids
- Phase 5: Select winner; negotiate SPA; sign
- Phase 6: Regulatory approval; close

**Timeline:** 6–18 months from kickoff to close (regulatory approvals are the wildcard in Asia)

### Asian Strategic Buyer Universe

**Korean strategic buyers:**
- Samsung Group (C&T, SDC, Electronics, BioLogics — depending on sector)
- Hyundai, SK, LG, Lotte, Hanwha — all active corporate M&A
- Korean mid-caps seeking market consolidation (especially construction, healthcare, services)

**Japanese strategic buyers:**
- Marubeni, Itochu, Sumitomo, Mitsui, Mitsubishi (trading houses — extremely active in cross-border M&A)
- Softbank, NTT, Fujitsu (tech-adjacent)
- Nidec, Keyence, Fanuc (precision industrials)

**Global strategics interested in Asia exposure:**
- US/European corporates seeking Asian distribution or production platforms
- Chinese state-owned enterprises (becoming more constrained for outbound M&A)

## 4. Secondary Buyout (SBO)

A secondary buyout is when a PE firm sells to another PE firm. Once stigmatized, SBOs have become a normal and often efficient exit route.

### When SBO Makes Sense

**For the seller (MBK):**
- Fund approaching end of life; need to return capital; company not yet ready for IPO
- Strategic buyer not available or antitrust issues prevent strategic sale
- Faster closing certainty than IPO or strategic process

**For the buyer (incoming PE firm):**
- Company has been operationally improved but still has further value creation runway (add-on M&A not completed; geographic expansion just starting)
- Entry at lower multiple than at IPO (SBO typically 0.5–1.5x below comparable IPO)
- New PE owner can add specific capability that the previous owner lacked

### SBO Valuation Dynamics

```
SBO Pricing:  Typically 0.5–1.5x EBITDA below comparable strategic/IPO
Why discount? - Company has already been PE-owned (known; less "discovery" upside)
              - Buyer must pay for future value creation runway that exists
              - Seller is often motivated (fund life pressure = price concession)

Seller's calculus:
  Strategic sale: 11.5x EBITDA
  IPO (expected): 11.0x EBITDA (but 12-18 month timeline + lock-up)
  SBO offer:      10.5x EBITDA (fast close, certain, no lock-up)
  → SBO often preferred if fund timeline is tight
```

## 5. Dividend Recapitalization (Mid-Hold Capital Return)

A dividend recap is not an exit — it is a mid-hold tool to return capital to LPs while retaining ownership of the asset.

**How it works:**
- Business has paid down debt and built equity value
- PE refinances the debt at higher leverage, extracting the incremental capacity as a dividend
- LPs receive a cash distribution before the full exit; IRR improves significantly

**Dividend recap math:**
```
Year 3 situation:
  EBITDA:          $130M (up from $100M at entry)
  Current debt:    $300M (3x EBITDA — significantly paid down from original $500M)
  Available to re-lever to 5x: 5x × $130M = $650M
  Incremental debt capacity:   $650M − $300M = $350M
  
  → Refinance to $650M; pay $350M dividend to PE fund
  → LPs receive $350M distribution on $400M equity check = 87% returned
  → Remaining hold on the equity is essentially a "free" option

IRR impact: Receiving 87% of capital back in Year 3 dramatically improves IRR even if exit multiple is lower
```

**When dividend recap is appropriate:**
- Business is meaningfully above initial EBITDA target
- Debt markets are open (spreads reasonable)
- Management is stable and not destabilized by re-leveraging
- LPs have upcoming capital needs (recaps help PE manage LP relationships)

**Risks of dividend recap:**
- Re-leverages the business; reduces resilience to downturn
- Can distract management ("did we just take out a big dividend?")
- Requires lender consent; not always available

## 6. Exit Timing Optimization

### The Optimal Hold Period: 3–5 Years

**Why 3–5 years is the sweet spot:**
- Year 1: Stabilization, 100-day plan, operational improvements just starting
- Year 2–3: Value creation flywheel in motion; EBITDA building toward target
- Year 4–5: Peak value realization; exit before growth slows and thesis gets stale
- Year 6+: Multiple compression risk; growth slows; management fatigue; LP pressure increases

**The danger of holding too long:**
```
Year 5:  EBITDA = $165M → exit at 10x = $1,650M EV
Year 7:  EBITDA = $190M → but growth slowing; multiple compresses to 8x = $1,520M EV
Year 7 equity: Lower despite higher EBITDA, due to multiple compression + more years of LP waiting
```

**Holding too long destroys IRR even when MOIC improves.** A 3.0x MOIC in 5 years (25% IRR) beats a 3.5x MOIC in 8 years (17% IRR).

### Exit Multiple Sensitivity

The exit multiple assumption is the single most dangerous number in the LBO model — it is the hardest to predict and has the largest impact on returns.

**Stress test the exit multiple aggressively:**

| Exit Multiple | Exit EBITDA $165M | Exit EV | Exit Equity ($175M debt) | MOIC |
|--------------|------------------|---------|--------------------------|------|
| 7.0x | $165M | $1,155M | $980M | 2.45x |
| 8.0x | $165M | $1,320M | $1,145M | 2.86x |
| 9.0x | $165M | $1,485M | $1,310M | 3.28x |
| 10.0x | $165M | $1,650M | $1,475M | 3.69x |
| 11.0x | $165M | $1,815M | $1,640M | 4.10x |

*Entry equity check assumed: $400M. Passes hurdle at 7.5x+ exit multiple.*

**Key principle:** Never underwrite an exit multiple above the entry multiple without specific justification (operational improvement, strategic positioning, market re-rating). Multiple expansion is a gift, not a plan.

## 7. Exit Preparation Checklist

Begin exit preparation 18–24 months before target exit:

**Financial preparation:**
- [ ] 3 years of audited financials (IFRS or GAAP; appropriate for target market)
- [ ] Quality of earnings report prepared (often re-run by sell-side advisor)
- [ ] Normalized EBITDA bridge clearly documented and defensible
- [ ] Management projections (3-year plan) reviewed and pressure-tested
- [ ] No pending restatements or audit issues

**Legal / corporate preparation:**
- [ ] All material contracts reviewed for change of control provisions
- [ ] IP registered and clean in all operating jurisdictions
- [ ] No material litigation outstanding
- [ ] Corporate structure simplified (eliminate holding company complexity)
- [ ] Data room prepared and organized

**Management preparation:**
- [ ] Retention packages for key management through close
- [ ] CEO/CFO comfortable presenting investment thesis to buyers
- [ ] Management equity plan terms clear (acceleration on change of control?)
- [ ] Succession plan for any management who will exit post-close

**Market preparation:**
- [ ] Identify bankers 18 months out; pitch 12 months out; mandate 9 months out
- [ ] Comparable company and transaction analysis completed
- [ ] Buyer universe mapped (strategic + PE) with preliminary outreach
- [ ] Timing: Avoid year-end and major market volatility periods
