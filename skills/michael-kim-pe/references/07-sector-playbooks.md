# Sector-Specific PE Playbooks

> "Sectors are not fungible. Software at 12x EBITDA is not the same as industrials at 7x. Each sector has distinct dynamics, risk factors, and value creation levers that must be understood before deploying capital."

---

## 1. Software / SaaS

**Typical Entry Multiple:** 8–12x revenue (for growth SaaS); 12–18x EBITDA for mature profitability

### Key Metrics and Diligence Framework

| Metric | High Quality Benchmark | Red Flag |
|--------|----------------------|----------|
| ARR Growth | > 30% YoY for venture-stage; > 15% for mature | < 10% growth in competitive market |
| Gross Margin | > 75% | < 60% (suggests heavy services component) |
| Net Revenue Retention | > 115% | < 100% (churn exceeding expansion) |
| Rule of 40 | Growth + FCF margin ≥ 40 | < 20% (poor unit economics) |
| CAC Payback | < 18 months | > 36 months (capital inefficient growth) |
| Logo Churn | < 10% annually | > 20% (product-market fit issues) |

### PE-Specific Considerations

**Revenue Recognition:**
- SaaS revenue is recurring but often annual upfront billing. Deferred revenue on balance sheet represents cash already collected but not yet earned.
- **Key metric:** ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue) is the valuation driver, not GAAP revenue.
- **Red flag:** Revenue recognition aggressive (multi-year deals recognized upfront vs. ratably).

**Customer Concentration:**
- SaaS businesses often have high customer concentration (top 5 customers = 30–60% of ARR is common).
- **Risk:** Land-and-expand models depend on expansion renewals; if top customer churns, ARR collapses.
- **Mitigation:** Track dollar-weighted retention separately from logo retention.

**R&D Spending:**
- Mature SaaS typically spends 15–25% of revenue on R&D.
- **PE value creation lever:** R&D productivity — is the company shipping features that drive ARR, or accumulating technical debt?
- **Cutting R&D is dangerous:** Reduces competitiveness; SaaS requires continuous product evolution.

**Rule of 40:**
- Formula: YoY ARR Growth % + FCF Margin % ≥ 40
- Example: 25% growth + 15% FCF margin = 40 (acceptable)
- Example: 40% growth + 0% FCF = 40 (acceptable — reinvesting for growth)
- Example: 10% growth + 10% FCF = 20 (not acceptable — neither growing nor profitable)

### Value Creation Playbook

**Revenue growth levers:**
1. **Sales efficiency:** Improve CAC by 20% through target account focus and better SDR targeting
2. **Land-and-expand:** Install customer success team to drive upsell/cross-sell
3. **Pricing power:** Annual price increases (5–10%) typical for SaaS; underutilized by most companies
4. **Geographic expansion:** Localize product and sales for Japan/Korea from US base

**Margin expansion:**
- Reduce services revenue (low margin) by productizing implementation
- Cloud infrastructure cost optimization (AWS/Azure negotiation; right-sizing instances)
- Offshore R&D to lower-cost locations while maintaining IP ownership

**Add-on strategy:**
- Complementary products that can be sold to existing customer base
- Vertical-specific modules (horizontal SaaS acquiring vertical SaaS)
- API-first companies that integrate into existing platform

## 2. Industrials / Manufacturing

**Typical Entry Multiple:** 6–9x EBITDA (lower for commodity businesses, higher for proprietary technology)

### Key Metrics and Diligence Framework

| Metric | High Quality Benchmark | Red Flag |
|--------|----------------------|----------|
| EBITDA Margin | > 12% (varies by subsector) | < 8% with no clear path to improvement |
| Asset Turns | > 1.0x (capital efficiency) | < 0.5x (asset-heavy, low returns) |
| Maintenance CapEx | 2–3% of revenue | > 5% (aging assets or poor maintenance discipline) |
| On-Time Delivery | > 95% | < 85% (indicates operational chaos) |
| Customer Concentration | Top 5 customers < 30% | Top 1 customer > 25% |
| Backlog to Revenue Ratio | > 1.0x (visibility) | < 0.3x (spot market, volatile) |

### PE-Specific Considerations

**Cyclicality Risk:**
- Industrial businesses are exposed to GDP, interest rates, and commodity cycles.
- **PE timing:** Avoid entry at cycle peak; look for troughs or mid-cycle stability.
- **Downside protection:** Backlog coverage, long-term contracts, recession-resistant end-markets (defense, healthcare, infrastructure).

**Capital Intensity:**
- Industrials typically require significant ongoing CapEx to maintain capacity.
- **Key metric:** ROIC (Return on Invested Capital). Should be > WACC + 5% to justify reinvestment.
- **PE value creation:** Working capital optimization (inventory turns, receivables acceleration) often more impactful than revenue growth.

**Supply Chain Complexity:**
- Many industrials have long supply chains with tier-n supplier dependencies.
- **Diligence focus:** Single-source dependencies, geographic concentration (China exposure), supplier financial health.
- **COVID lesson:** Supply chain resilience is now valued; nearshoring/reshoring creates margin expansion opportunities.

### Value Creation Playbook

**Operational improvement:**
1. **Lean manufacturing:** Implement continuous improvement (Kaizen) programs; typical 2–5% cost reduction
2. **Procurement consolidation:** Aggregate spend across business units; renegotiate with scale advantage
3. **Energy efficiency:** LED conversion, compressed air optimization, HVAC upgrades (quick payback)
4. **Plant footprint optimization:** Consolidate overlapping facilities; exit low-margin geographies

**Commercial improvement:**
- Pricing discipline: Move from cost-plus to value-based pricing where proprietary
- Channel optimization: Reduce distributor dependence; build direct sales for strategic accounts
- Aftermarket services: Service contracts, spare parts (higher margin than OEM equipment)

**Add-on strategy:**
- Vertical integration: Acquire key suppliers or distributors
- Geographic expansion: Use platform as beachhead for bolt-on targets
- Capability expansion: Add adjacent manufacturing processes

## 3. Healthcare Services

**Typical Entry Multiple:** 10–14x EBITDA (regulatory moat, recurring demand, defensive characteristics)

### Key Metrics and Diligence Framework

| Metric | High Quality Benchmark | Red Flag |
|--------|----------------------|----------|
| EBITDA Margin | > 15% | < 10% with reimbursement pressure |
| Same-Store Growth | > 3% annually | < 0% (market share loss) |
| Patient/Client Concentration | Top 5 < 20% | Top 1 > 15% |
| Payer Mix | Diversified (commercial, Medicare, Medicaid, self-pay) | > 60% single payer type |
| Clinical Quality Metrics | Above 75th percentile | Regulatory investigations or sanctions |
| Staff Turnover | < 15% annually | > 30% (indicates culture/working conditions issues) |

### PE-Specific Considerations

**Regulatory Moat:**
- Healthcare services require licenses, certifications, and regulatory approvals that create barriers to entry.
- **Opportunity:** Regulatory complexity keeps out competitors; quality players consolidate fragmented markets.
- **Risk:** Regulatory changes (reimbursement cuts, scope-of-practice rules) can abruptly impact economics.

**Reimbursement Risk:**
- Many healthcare services depend on government (Medicare/Medicaid) or commercial insurance reimbursement.
- **Diligence:** Analyze historical reimbursement rates; model cuts in downside case.
- **Korean/Japanese context:** National health insurance systems create price ceilings; volume growth is the primary driver.

**Clinical Quality and Compliance:**
- Healthcare services have regulatory scrutiny (medical board, ministry of health).
- **Red flags:** Past violations; pending investigations; high complaint rates; insurance claims disputes.

### Value Creation Playbook

**Revenue growth:**
1. **De novo expansion:** Open new facilities in adjacent geographies (proven model, lower entry multiple than acquisition)
2. **Service line expansion:** Add complementary services to existing locations
3. **Payer contracting:** Negotiate better rates with commercial insurers; improve mix toward higher-reimbursement services
4. **Marketing and patient acquisition:** Digital marketing, physician referral networks

**Margin expansion:**
- Labor optimization: Right-size staffing ratios; reduce agency/overtime usage
- Supply chain: Group purchasing organization (GPO) participation
- Revenue cycle management: Reduce days to collect; improve coding accuracy

**Add-on strategy:**
- Same-city consolidation: Buy competitors to gain density and negotiating leverage
- Vertical integration: Acquire complementary service providers along care continuum

## 4. Consumer / Retail

**Typical Entry Multiple:** 6–10x EBITDA (brand-dependent; e-commerce disruptors trade at premiums)

### Key Metrics and Diligence Framework

| Metric | High Quality Benchmark | Red Flag |
|--------|----------------------|----------|
| Same-Store Sales Growth | > 3% | < 0% (brand fatigue or structural decline) |
| Gross Margin | > 50% (brand/retail) | < 30% (commodity exposure) |
| Inventory Turns | > 4x annually | < 2x (fashion risk, obsolescence) |
| Contribution Margin by SKU | > 60% of SKUs profitable | 20%+ SKUs loss-making but "carried for assortment" |
| E-commerce Penetration | Growing share of total | Stagnant or declining (missing channel shift) |
| Brand Awareness (unaided) | Top 2 in category | Not spontaneously mentioned |

### PE-Specific Considerations

**E-commerce Disruption:**
- Physical retail is under severe pressure from e-commerce in most categories.
- **PE lens:** Does the target have a credible omnichannel strategy? Is e-commerce growing faster than physical?
- **Amazon effect:** If the product category is on Amazon, the brand must have differentiation beyond price.

**Brand Value vs. Brand Risk:**
- Brand creates pricing power but also requires continuous marketing investment.
- **Key metric:** Marketing spend as % of revenue. Typical: 5–15% for CPG.
- **Value creation trap:** Cutting marketing to improve short-term EBITDA destroys brand equity and long-term value.

**Inventory Risk:**
- Fashion, seasonal, and trend-driven businesses carry high inventory write-off risk.
- **Diligence:** Historical inventory write-offs; aged inventory analysis; markdown policies.

### Value Creation Playbook

**Revenue growth:**
1. **E-commerce acceleration:** Invest in digital capabilities; marketplace presence (Tmall, Amazon, Coupang)
2. **Geographic expansion:** Enter new markets through distributors or owned retail
3. **Product innovation:** New SKUs in adjacent categories; limited editions to drive excitement
4. **Private label:** Develop exclusive products with higher margins

**Margin expansion:**
- SKU rationalization: Eliminate tail SKUs that dilute margin and tie up working capital
- Direct-to-consumer shift: Higher margin than wholesale; capture customer data
- Supply chain optimization: Factory consolidation; nearshoring to reduce lead times

**Add-on strategy:**
- Brand portfolio: Acquire complementary brands that leverage shared infrastructure
- Distribution: Acquire distributors in geographies where brand has low penetration
- Vertical integration: Acquire manufacturing capability (if currently outsourced)

## 5. Financial Services

**Typical Entry Multiple:** 1.5–2.5x book value (P/B) or 8–12x earnings; asset management: 8–15x EBITDA varies by AUM

### Key Metrics and Diligence Framework

| Metric | High Quality Benchmark | Red Flag |
|--------|----------------------|----------|
| Return on Equity (ROE) | > 15% | < 10% (cost of equity not covered) |
| Net Interest Margin (NIM) | > 2.5% | < 1.5% (compressed spread) |
| Cost-to-Income Ratio | < 50% | > 65% (overhead bloated) |
| NPL Ratio | < 2% | > 5% (credit quality deterioration) |
| CET1 Ratio (banks) | > 12% | < 8% (regulatory pressure) |
| AUM Growth (asset managers) | > 8% annually | Net outflows for 2+ consecutive years |

### PE-Specific Considerations

**Regulatory Capital:**
- Banks and insurers have minimum capital requirements that constrain leverage.
- **PE limitation:** Cannot use typical LBO leverage on banks; must use "bank holding company" structures with minority stakes.
- **Non-bank financial services:** More attractive for PE (less capital-intensive).

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:**
- Rising rates generally benefit banks (NIM expansion) but hurt asset managers (fixed income AUM values decline).
- **Timing:** Model rate scenarios explicitly; financial services earnings are highly sensitive to yield curve shape.

**Fintech Disruption:**
- Traditional financial services face disruption from fintech and neobanks.
- **PE lens:** Does the target have credible digital capabilities? Is it acquiring or being disrupted?

### Value Creation Playbook

**Revenue growth:**
1. **Product expansion:** Cross-sell additional services to existing customer base
2. **Channel digitization:** Reduce branch/physical costs; migrate to digital
3. **Fee income focus:** Shift from interest-dependent to fee-based revenue
4. **Niche specialization:** Focus on underserved segments (SME lending, specialized asset classes)

**Margin expansion:**
- Technology modernization: Core banking system upgrades reduce operating costs
- Branch rationalization: Consolidate overlapping locations in acquired portfolios
- Operating leverage: Scale fixed costs over larger asset base

**Add-on strategy (asset management):**
- AUM rollup: Combine multiple smaller managers to achieve scale
- Capability expansion: Add asset classes (private credit, real assets) through acquisition
- Distribution reach: Acquire distribution relationships in new geographies

## Sector Selection Heuristics for MBK-Style PE

**Prefer sectors with:**
- Defensible market position (fragmented local leaders with brand or regulatory barriers)
- Recurring revenue (subscriptions, contracts, maintenance vs. project/spot)
- Asset-light or asset-right (high ROIC, low maintenance CapEx)
- Asian growth exposure (aging demographics favor healthcare; middle class growth favors consumer)

**Avoid sectors with:**
- Rapid technology disruption (unless PE thesis is specifically disruption-driven)
- Structural decline in end-market demand
- Commodity exposure without cost advantage
- Heavy regulatory risk without corresponding moat
