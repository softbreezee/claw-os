# Governance Analysis: Identifying Activist Opportunities

> **When to read this file**: when evaluating whether a company's governance failures create an activist opportunity, analyzing board composition for independence problems, assessing CEO compensation alignment, identifying entrenchment mechanisms that block shareholder value, or pre-assessing whether ISS/Glass Lewis would support an activist campaign.

---

## The Governance-Value Link

Elliott's core insight: **Most value gaps are not caused by bad markets — they are caused by bad governance.** A board that fails to hold management accountable, a CEO whose pay is misaligned with shareholder returns, a structure that entrenches management against change — these governance failures compound over time into massive value destruction.

> "The board is the ultimate check on management. When the board fails, shareholders suffer." — Elliott Management governance analysis framework

> "Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome. Good governance aligns incentives; bad governance misaligns them." — Adapted from Munger, frequently cited by Elliott

**The Five Governance Failure Patterns Elliott Targets**:
1. **Board Capture** — The board works for the CEO, not for shareholders
2. **Compensation Misalignment** — Pay rewards size/growth over value creation
3. **Entrenchment** — Poison pills, staggered boards, dual-class shares that insulate management from accountability
4. **Succession Failure** — No credible CEO succession plan, or the wrong successor was chosen
5. **Capital Allocation Absence** — Board never challenges management's capital deployment decisions

## Module 1: Board Composition Analysis

### The Independence Calculation

**Standard requirement**: For NYSE/NASDAQ listing, a majority of directors must be independent. But Elliott applies a stricter test.

**Elliott's Enhanced Independence Test**:
```
Step 1: Check regulatory independence (NYSE/NASDAQ rules)
  - Director not employed by company in past 3 years ✓
  - No material commercial relationship (>$120K) ✓
  - Not related to an executive ✓

Step 2: Check "social independence" (Elliott's critical add)
  - Did director serve under current CEO before they were CEO? (Former subordinate → captured)
  - Does director serve on multiple boards with CEO or other directors? (Social network capture)
  - Is director's primary compensation from other board positions dependent on CEO referral? (Referral network)
  - Has director ever voted against management in a contested election? (Independence demonstrated)

Step 3: Check "tenure decay"
  - Tenure >10 years on same board → independence decay (relationship deepening)
  - Tenure >12 years → high correlation with governance failure
  - Tenure 0-3 years → highest independence (new relationship hasn't formed)

Scoring:
  "Truly Independent" = Regulatory independent + Social independent + Tenure <12 years
  "Captured" = Regulatory independent but fails social independence test
  "Affiliated" = Regulatory non-independent
```

**The Target Pattern**: When "Truly Independent" directors are <40% of board, governance risk is high.

### Board Skill Matrix Audit

Elliott evaluates whether the board has the skills to oversee the company:

| Skill Area | Required? | Current Board Coverage | Gap? |
|------------|-----------|----------------------|------|
| Industry/operational expertise | Yes | [X/Y directors] | [Yes/No] |
| M&A / strategic transactions | For acquisitive companies | [X/Y directors] | [Yes/No] |
| Capital allocation / finance | Yes | [X/Y directors] | [Yes/No] |
| Technology / transformation | For digital businesses | [X/Y directors] | [Yes/No] |
| Governance / shareholder relations | For contested situations | [X/Y directors] | [Yes/No] |

**Red flag**: Board lacks industry expertise in a company undergoing industry transformation. Example: Retail board without e-commerce expertise during digital shift.

### The Lead Independent Director Assessment

In US boards, the Lead Independent Director (LID) is the key governance figure when CEO is also Chairman.

**Elliott's LID Evaluation**:
- Does LID set the board agenda independently, or does the CEO control it?
- Does LID meet with shareholders independently, or only with CEO present?
- Does LID have authority to call executive sessions without CEO?
- If NO to any of these → the LID is ceremonial, not functional.

## Module 2: CEO Compensation Alignment Analysis

### The Pay-for-Performance Test

**Elliott's Core Question**: If the stock underperforms the S&P 500 by 20% over 3 years, does the CEO's total compensation also decline by 20%?

**The Misalignment Patterns**:

**Pattern 1: Revenue Growth Reward Without Profitability**
```
CEO compensation formula: 60% of bonus tied to revenue growth
Problem: CEO can grow revenue via bad acquisitions that destroy value
Elliott's fix: Weight compensation 50% toward TSR (total shareholder return) vs. peers, 30% toward ROIC, 20% toward revenue only if margins maintained
```

**Pattern 2: Option Grant Timing**
```
CEO receives options annually at 52-week low → asymmetric upside
Elliott's fix: Options granted at fixed quarterly intervals, or relative to peer TSR performance hurdles
```

**Pattern 3: Change-in-Control Protection**
```
CEO has 3x salary + bonus "golden parachute" with no performance condition
Elliott view: Severance should be 1-2x max, and only if deal delivers premium to pre-announcement price
The real problem: CEO is incentivized to sell company at ANY price to trigger the payout
```

**Pattern 4: Pay Ratio**
```
CEO pay > 100x median employee pay AND below-median TSR vs. peers
Elliott's calculation: Compare CEO pay ratio vs. 3-year TSR percentile rank
  If CEO is top-decile pay but bottom-decile TSR → governance failure
```

### The Compensation Scorecard

| Metric | Current | Peer Median | Alignment? |
|--------|---------|-------------|------------|
| CEO total comp | $XXM | $YYM | [Aligned/High/Low] |
| 3-year TSR vs. S&P 500 | +X% | N/A | [Outperform/Underperform] |
| Pay-for-performance sensitivity | $X per 1% TSR | $Y per 1% TSR | [Appropriate/Too low] |
| Golden parachute | $XXM (3x) | $YYM (2x) | [Excessive/Appropriate] |
| Equity vs. cash mix | X%/Y% | A%/B% | [Aligned misaligned] |

## Module 3: Entrenchment Mechanisms

### The Anti-Takeover Arsenal

Elliott identifies and targets these mechanisms:

**1. Staggered (Classified) Boards**
```
Structure: Board divided into 3 classes, 1/3 elected each year
Effect: Minimum 2 years to gain board majority even with 100% shareholder support
Elliott's view: Entrenchment mechanism that should be eliminated
Success rate of declassification campaigns: ~65% (ISS strongly supports declassification)

Target: Companies with staggered boards + below-median TSR are prime activist targets
```

**2. Poison Pills (Shareholder Rights Plans)**
```
Structure: If any shareholder crosses X% without board approval, all other shareholders can buy shares at 50% discount — diluting the "hostile" acquirer to irrelevance
Elliott's view: Pills are defensible for 6-12 months to allow board to seek alternatives, but "dead-hand" pills (stays in effect for new board) are abusive

When to target: Pills with threshold <15% (too low), or pills with >3 year duration, or any dead-hand provision
```

**3. Dual-Class Share Structures**
```
Structure: Class A shares = 1 vote, Class B shares = 10 votes (held by founder/insiders)
Effect: Insiders control 50%+ of votes with <20% economic ownership
Elliott's view: Dual-class is structurally anti-shareholder; only acceptable if sunset provision (converts to single-class after X years or upon transfer)
Campaign approach: Demand sunset provision, or at minimum equal voting rights for holders >5 years

Red flag: No sunset provision + below-market TSR = unaccountable management
```

**4. Supermajority Voting Requirements**
```
Structure: Mergers, charter amendments, or removal of directors require 66-80% approval
Effect: Minority blocking position can prevent change
Elliott's view: 50% + 1 standard is sufficient; supermajority is entrenchment

Target: Supermajority requirement + family/insider 25-35% block = minority veto power
```

### Entrenchment Score Calculation

```
Staggered board: +3 points (severe entrenchment)
Poison pill in place: +2 points
Dual-class shares without sunset: +3 points (impossible to overcome)
Supermajority requirements: +2 points
Director removal only for cause: +1 point

Score interpretation:
  0-2: Low entrenchment, board can be changed via standard proxy contest
  3-5: Medium entrenchment, requires multi-year campaign or settlement pressure
  6+: High entrenchment, campaign likely to fail via proxy alone; must target different pressure points
```

## Module 4: Shareholder Base Analysis

### The Ownership Profile Assessment

Elliott evaluates who owns the stock before launching a campaign:

| Ownership Type | Friendly to Activism? | Why |
|----------------|---------------------|-----|
| Index funds (Vanguard, BlackRock, State Street) | Neutral-Supportive | Vote with ISS; governance-focused |
| Active managers (Fidelity, T. Rowe, Capital Group) | Variable | Depends on fund manager relationship with target |
| Hedge funds | Supportive if thesis aligns | Often join coalition |
| Pension funds (CalPERS, CalSTRS, NYC) | Very supportive | Active governance participants |
| Sovereign wealth funds | Neutral | Generally passive |
| Family/founders | Hostile | Will vote with entrenched management |
| Corporate insiders | Hostile | Voting with their jobs |

**Elliott's pre-campaign question**: What percentage of shares is held by institutional investors with a stated policy of supporting shareholder proposals?

**The 25% Rule**: If institutions with pro-shareholder governance policies hold >25% of shares, an activist campaign has a base level of support that makes it winnable.

### The Float Analysis

**Free float** = Shares outstanding - Insiders - Strategic holders - Index fund auto-votes

**Example**:
```
Shares outstanding: 400M
Insiders/founders: 80M (20%) — votes with management
Strategic holders: 40M (10%) — likely neutral or with management
Index funds: 120M (30%) — vote with ISS (variable)
Free float: 160M (40%)

Elliott needs: 50% of votes in a proxy fight = 200M shares
Assuming ISS supports Elliott (index funds follow): 120M + 160M × X% = 200M
Required free float support: 80M / 160M = 50% of remaining shareholders

Conclusion: Winnable with ISS support; requires 2-3 other large institutions to join coalition
```

## Module 5: ISS/Glass Lewis Pre-Assessment

### How ISS Evaluates Governance

**ISS Score Components** (simplified for Elliott's purposes):
1. **Board Structure** (25%): Independence, tenure, diversity
2. **Compensation** (30%): Pay-for-performance alignment, equity mix, golden parachutes
3. **Shareholder Rights** (25%): Staggered boards, poison pills, dual-class, supermajority
4. **Audit** (10%): Auditor independence, fees, related party transactions
5. **ESG** (10%): Disclosure, policies (not deeply evaluated historically)

**ISS Quick Score Thresholds**:
- ISS Score >7/10: Likely to support management in proxy fight
- ISS Score 4-6/10: Swing state — campaign specific
- ISS Score <4/10: Likely to support activist campaign

### The ISS Campaign Playbook

**Before going public**, Elliott does ISS pre-work:
1. **Engage ISS analysts privately** (common practice) — present the governance concerns without revealing full campaign
2. **Submit governance white papers** — document the board's failures with specific data
3. **Propose specific fixes** — ISS likes activists who offer solutions, not just complaints
4. **Highlight peer comparisons** — "Company X has governance score of 3/10 while peers average 7/10"

**ISS Red Flags Elliott Exploits**:
- Directors with >15 years tenure (ISS "freshness" standard)
- CEO is also Chairman with no lead independent director
- Poison pill with no sunset provision
- Related-party transactions with directors
- Earnings restatements during director's tenure

## Governance Failure Identification Checklist

Use this checklist to assess whether governance creates an activist opportunity:

- [ ] **Board independence**: <50% truly independent directors (Elliott's social independence test)
- [ ] **Tenure problem**: >40% of directors with >10 years tenure
- [ ] **CEO duality**: CEO is also Chairman with no functional lead independent director
- [ ] **Compensation misalignment**: CEO pay increased while TSR underperformed peers
- [ ] **Golden parachute**: Severance >2x salary + bonus with no performance condition
- [ ] **Staggered board**: Board is classified (1/3 elected annually)
- [ ] **Poison pill**: Rights plan in effect with <15% threshold or dead-hand provision
- [ ] **Dual-class shares**: Unequal voting rights with no sunset provision
- [ ] **Supermajority**: Mergers or charter changes require >66% approval
- [ ] **Related-party transactions**: Directors have material commercial relationships with company
- [ ] **No succession plan**: No disclosed CEO succession planning process
- [ ] **Capital allocation rubber stamp**: Board has never rejected management M&A proposal

**Scoring**:
- 0-3 checks: Governance is adequate — not an activist target on governance grounds
- 4-6 checks: Governance concerns present — may support other campaign demands
- 7+ checks: Severe governance failure — standalone activist opportunity
