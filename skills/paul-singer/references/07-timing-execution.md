# Timing & Execution: When and How to Launch Activist Campaigns

> **When to read this file:** When assessing whether market conditions and company-specific events create a favorable moment to initiate or escalate an activist campaign, when planning the sequence of position-building and public disclosure, when evaluating proxy mechanics and institutional shareholder receptivity, or when mapping the typical timeline from initial investment to campaign resolution.

The best activist thesis is worthless if launched at the wrong moment. Elliott's edge is not just identifying governance failures and valuation gaps — it is the precise calibration of *when* to enter, *when* to go public, and *when* to escalate. A campaign launched too early (before the company's failures are undeniable to third parties) or too late (after a recovery rally closes the discount) will fail regardless of thesis quality. Timing and execution are the operational core of Elliott's activist methodology.

> "Activism is not just analysis. It is strategy, timing, and execution. You can be right about everything and still lose a proxy fight because you miscalculated the shareholder base or moved before the company had sufficiently alienated its investors. The art is knowing when to move." — Elliott Management, internal campaign post-mortem framework

> "We are not raiders. We are catalysts. We enter when the conditions are right for change — when management has lost credibility, when the board has run out of excuses, and when the shareholder base is ready to listen to an alternative." — Elliott Management

## Module 1: Optimal Entry Conditions

### The Four High-Probability Entry Windows

Elliott's campaigns are disproportionately initiated within four specific company condition windows. These windows share a common feature: management credibility has been damaged but not fully destroyed, the stock is depressed against intrinsic value, and shareholders are questioning whether change is needed.

**Window 1: After Earnings Disappointment (Especially Sequential Misses)**

A single earnings miss may reflect normal business volatility. Three consecutive misses — particularly when accompanied by guidance reductions — indicate structural problems that management has either failed to diagnose correctly or is unable to communicate honestly.

**Why this creates an opportunity:**
- Institutional shareholders have reduced positions (lower cost basis for activist entry)
- Sell-side analysts have begun downgrading (ISS/Glass Lewis now view TSR unfavorably)
- Management explanations have shifted from "transient" to "structural" (credibility declining)
- The stock price embeds pessimism — Elliott's acquisition is at a discount to potential

**Elliott's entry signal checklist for earnings disappointments:**
- [ ] Three or more consecutive quarters of earnings below consensus
- [ ] Guidance reductions in at least two of the last four quarters
- [ ] Stock has underperformed the sector index by >15% over trailing 12 months
- [ ] Sell-side consensus has shifted from Buy to Hold/Neutral on most models
- [ ] Management's explanation for misses has evolved from specific to generic ("macro headwinds")

**Window 2: After Failed M&A or Capital Allocation Mistake**

When management pursues an acquisition or strategic pivot that destroys value, the resulting credibility loss creates a window for activist pressure. The market's negative reaction to a capital allocation decision reveals that the board has failed its oversight function.

**High-value failed M&A patterns:**
- Acquirer overpays by >30% vs. peer deal multiples (deal was "empire building")
- Acquisition in a business adjacent to but not synergistic with core competency
- "Transformative" deal that diversifies away from the company's highest-ROIC segment
- Leveraged acquisition that constrains balance sheet at a cyclical peak

> "When a company overpays for an acquisition, three things happen: the stock goes down on announcement (the market is smarter than the board), the integration fails to deliver synergies (management knew but didn't care), and the CEO asks for patience (the board capitulates). That's when we move." — Elliott campaign strategy framework

**Window 3: During CEO Transitions**

CEO transitions — particularly when the outgoing CEO was personally identified with a failing strategy — create a narrow window where the board is temporarily open to external input.

**Why CEO transitions favor activists:**
- The incoming CEO has no ownership of the prior strategy (more receptive to change)
- The board is scrutinizing its own past decisions (internal accountability moment)
- Shareholders are withholding judgment, creating a "blank slate" narrative that activists can shape
- The transition creates natural events (strategic reviews, capital allocation announcements) that activists can influence

**The optimal CEO transition entry:**
- **Before announcement of successor**: Build position while transition is announced but successor unknown; the board is most vulnerable in this window
- **First 90 days of new CEO**: New leadership wants to establish credibility — will often accept activist demands as "new strategic direction"
- **Avoid**: Wait-and-see period after new CEO announces strategy (window closes; board rallies around new leader)

**Window 4: Sustained Underperformance vs. Peers**

When a company has delivered below-median TSR vs. sector peers over 3-5 years, the institutional shareholder base has largely lost patience. This creates structural support for activist campaigns.

**Elliott's peer underperformance threshold:**
```
3-year TSR vs. sector median: -15% or more → activist-favorable conditions
5-year TSR vs. sector median: -25% or more → strong activist-favorable conditions
Annual TSR while CEO has served: below cost of equity → CEO accountability question

Peer comparison must be sector-specific:
  - Use the 5-10 closest public comparables by revenue size and business mix
  - Adjust for spinoffs and acquisitions that change comparability
  - Weight by market cap of comparables (large peers matter more for ISS analysis)
```

## Module 2: Building the Position

### Phase 1: Passive Accumulation (Below 5%)

Elliott begins accumulating stock before triggering disclosure requirements, typically building to 4.9% of outstanding shares. This window allows position-building at undisclosed prices.

**Execution framework for passive accumulation:**
- Use multiple prime brokers to avoid concentrating order flow (reduces market impact)
- Accumulate over 60-90 days to average entry price and avoid front-running
- Combine open-market purchases with derivatives (call options, swaps) to access economic exposure without triggering immediate ownership reports
- Monitor the filing obligations for derivatives (notional exposure can trigger HSR thresholds)

**Cost basis discipline:**
The entry price at this phase determines the fund's entire economics. Elliott targets entry prices that imply at least 30-50% upside to a realistically achievable intrinsic value, even without a full activist premium.

**The passive accumulation "no-touch" rules:**
- Do not contact management or directors during this phase (creates "affiliate" status issues)
- Do not publicly comment on the company
- Do not participate in any investor meetings that could be construed as "group" activity

### Phase 2: 13D Threshold and HSR Filing

**13D disclosure** (the activist filing):
- Required within 10 days of crossing 5% beneficial ownership (SEC Rule 13d-1)
- 13D (vs. 13G) required when intent is to influence management or control
- Filing is public immediately upon submission to EDGAR
- The 13D filing itself is a campaign tool — the "purpose of the transaction" section communicates Elliott's demands

**What the 13D purpose section accomplishes:**
- Formally notifies management and board of Elliott's intentions
- Puts the investment community on notice (institutional shareholders pay attention)
- Creates a legal record that Elliott sought engagement before escalation
- Forces management to respond publicly to stated concerns

**HSR filing (Hart-Scott-Rodino)**:
- Required when position value exceeds $111.4M (2024 threshold, adjusted annually) AND company has sufficient revenues/assets
- Filing triggers a 30-day waiting period before additional purchases can be made
- HSR filing is a public signal that Elliott is building a significant position
- Elliott typically waits until HSR filing is imminent before going fully public (prevents management from implementing defenses before full position is established)

**The 13D → HSR sequencing:**
```
Day 1-70:    Passive accumulation (sub-5%)
Day 71:      Cross 5% → 13D filed within 10 days (public signal)
Day 71-90:   Continue accumulation, prepare for HSR if approaching threshold
Day 90:      HSR filing if position approaching reportable threshold
Day 91-120:  30-day HSR waiting period (no purchases)
Day 121+:    Full position established; public campaign begins
```

## Module 3: Proxy Campaign Mechanics

### The ISS/Glass Lewis Relationship

For most institutional shareholders, the vote in a proxy contest is determined by ISS (Institutional Shareholder Services) and Glass Lewis recommendations. Understanding how these proxy advisory firms evaluate campaigns is not optional — it is the central tactical question.

**ISS evaluation criteria for director elections in contested situations:**

| ISS Factor | Weight | Elliott's Approach |
|------------|--------|-------------------|
| Nominee quality/qualifications | High | Nominate highly credentialed candidates with direct industry experience |
| Activist's track record | High | Present documented campaign case studies with realized value creation |
| Company's response to concerns | High | Document management's refusal to engage constructively |
| Specific proposals/demands | High | Articulate concrete, measurable value creation steps |
| Board's existing governance quality | Medium | Leverage governance analysis (see 06-governance-analysis.md) |
| Campaign tone/constructiveness | Medium | Avoid personal attacks; focus on structural governance failures |

**The ISS campaign briefing:**

Elliott typically engages ISS analysts 60-90 days before the proxy vote:
1. Provide detailed governance white paper with specific data on board failures
2. Present financial analysis showing TSR gap vs. peers and intrinsic value discount
3. Introduce the slate of nominees personally (ISS interviews candidates)
4. Respond formally to management's opposition white paper
5. Request a formal recommendation meeting with ISS senior analysts

**What ISS typically supports:**
- Declassifying staggered boards (>65% success rate with ISS support)
- Majority voting for director elections
- Say-on-pay opposition when pay significantly exceeds peer levels without performance
- Separating Chairman/CEO roles at underperforming companies
- Replacing directors with >15 years tenure at underperforming companies

**What Glass Lewis adds:**
Glass Lewis is generally more sympathetic to activists than ISS, particularly when:
- The company has a pattern of governance failures (captured board, compensation excess)
- The activist has made specific, concrete proposals (not just general value complaints)
- The company has refused to engage or has engaged in bad faith

### Universal Proxy Cards

The SEC's universal proxy rule (effective September 2022) fundamentally changed proxy contest mechanics:

**Before universal proxy**: Shareholders had to choose between management's slate and the activist's slate. Mixing and matching was procedurally difficult.

**With universal proxy**: All director candidates (management nominees + activist nominees) appear on a single card. Shareholders can vote for any combination.

**How universal proxy changed Elliott's strategy:**

- **More selective campaigns**: Elliott can nominate 2-3 highly targeted directors rather than a full competing slate
- **Lower adversarial threshold**: A partial board change is easier to achieve without triggering management's full defensive resources
- **Better for minority activist positions**: Even a 5-8% holder can credibly campaign for 1-2 board seats
- **Forces management to run candidates on merit**: Management can no longer hide weak nominees behind a unified slate

**Elliott's universal proxy playbook:**
- Nominate the minimum number of directors needed to achieve the governance objective (often 2-3)
- Choose nominees with specific skills the current board lacks and the company clearly needs
- Position nominees as "additive" not "adversarial" — they are filling gaps, not staging a takeover
- Target the weakest incumbent directors for replacement (longest tenure + worst performance oversight)

### The Proxy Timeline

```
Months 1-2:    Position building and initial private engagement with management
Month 3:       13D filing; first formal letter to board
Months 3-4:    Private negotiation period (offer settlement before going public)
Month 4:       If no settlement, public campaign launch (white paper, press release)
Months 4-5:    Nominate director slate (if proxy contest); meet with institutional shareholders
Months 5-6:    ISS/Glass Lewis engagement; opposition white papers from both sides
Month 6-7:     Annual meeting / proxy vote
Months 7-18:   Post-vote board seat(s) exercise influence; monitor value creation milestones
```

## Module 4: Shareholder Base Analysis

### Mapping the Receptivity Landscape

Before launching any public campaign, Elliott maps the existing shareholder base into receptivity categories:

| Shareholder Type | Voting Behavior | Elliott's Approach |
|-----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Large active managers** (Fidelity, T. Rowe, Capital Group) | Case-by-case; relationship-driven | Private meetings 60-90 days before vote |
| **Index funds** (Vanguard, BlackRock, State Street "Big Three") | Follow ISS/Glass Lewis; governance-focused | Focus on ISS; the Big Three follow ISS in >75% of contested elections |
| **Pension funds with governance mandates** (CalPERS, TIAA) | Activist-sympathetic; TSR-focused | Engage directly; share governance analysis |
| **Hedge funds / other activists** | Often supportive if thesis aligns | Build coalition; share thesis (carefully — disclosure rules apply) |
| **Retail shareholders** | Generally follow management; small aggregate impact | Don't spend significant campaign resources |
| **Insiders / founders** | Reliably hostile | Count as management votes; calculate remaining swing vote needed |
| **Passive quasi-index** (DFA, dimensional) | Follow ISS | No direct engagement needed; maintain ISS focus |

**The 40% calculation:**

Elliott enters proxy contests only when analysis shows that achievable swing votes can reach 40-50%+ of votes cast. The calculation:

```
Total shares outstanding:              100%
Management controlled votes:           -15% (insiders + golden share)
ISS-following institutions:            +30% (if ISS supports Elliott)
CalPERS-style activist-friendly:       +5%
Hedge fund coalition:                  +5%
Remaining swing (large active managers): +10%
                                       ----
Achievable vote %:                     55% → Proxy contest is winnable
```

**If management controls >40% of votes** (founders, dual-class, concentrated strategic stakes) → proxy contest is structurally unwinnable → shift strategy to: regulatory pressure, public embarrassment, litigation, or sell the position.

### Private Engagement Before Going Public

Elliott's standard practice: offer management and the board a private resolution before any public campaign. This serves multiple purposes:

1. **Legal protection**: Demonstrates good faith; makes management's later "unreasonable activist" narrative harder to sustain
2. **Settlement efficiency**: Many campaigns resolve privately (avoiding proxy fight cost and disruption)
3. **ISS perception**: ISS looks more favorably on activists who engaged privately before escalating
4. **Intelligence gathering**: Management's response reveals whether they understand the problems and are willing to address them

**The private engagement framework:**
- First contact: Phone call from senior Elliott partner to Chairman or Lead Independent Director (not CEO)
- Written proposal: Detailed letter articulating governance failures, valuation analysis, and specific requests
- Response window: Give 30-60 days for meaningful response before escalating
- Settlement conditions: Board seats, strategic review, capital allocation commitments, governance reforms

> "We prefer private resolution. A proxy fight costs $10-20M and consumes 6-12 months of management bandwidth. If a company agrees to two board seats and a buyback program privately, that's better for everyone. We only go to war when the board refuses to acknowledge problems that are undeniable." — Elliott campaign principles

## Module 5: Campaign Timeline and Value Realization

### The 6-18 Month Typical Campaign Arc

```
PHASE 1: ENTRY AND INTELLIGENCE (Months 1-3)
  - Position accumulation (passive, below 5%)
  - Deep-dive governance and financial analysis
  - Shareholder base mapping
  - Preliminary contact with shareholder relations
  - 13D filing marks end of this phase

PHASE 2: PRIVATE ENGAGEMENT (Months 3-5)
  - Board-level private meetings
  - Written proposals submitted formally
  - Negotiation over settlement terms
  - If settlement reached → skip to Phase 5
  - If not → proceed to Phase 3

PHASE 3: PUBLIC ESCALATION (Months 5-7)
  - Public campaign launch: white paper, 13D amendment, press releases
  - Director nomination (if applicable)
  - Institutional shareholder road show (75-100 meetings in 8-10 weeks)
  - ISS and Glass Lewis engagement
  - Management responds with opposition materials

PHASE 4: PROXY VOTE (Month 7-8)
  - Proxy vote at annual or special meeting
  - Elliott wins seats → proceed to Phase 5
  - Elliott loses vote → reassess; may continue campaign or exit

PHASE 5: VALUE CREATION MONITORING (Months 8-18)
  - Board representatives influence strategic decisions
  - Monitor capital allocation (buybacks, dividends, M&A)
  - Track operational improvements (margin expansion, cost reduction)
  - Measure TSR progress vs. prior underperformance baseline
  - Evaluate exit: full thesis realization, partial, or strategic sale

PHASE 6: EXIT (Months 12-36 post-entry)
  - Full sale if intrinsic value achieved
  - Partial sale if position becomes too large relative to float
  - Remain invested if catalysts ongoing and upside remains
```

### When Campaigns End Early

Not all campaigns run to the proxy vote. Elliott exits or settles under these conditions:

**Early favorable settlement (most common outcome):**
- Management agrees to board seats + capital allocation commitment
- Settlement reached within 60-90 days of 13D
- Elliott typically requires: signed settlement agreement with specific milestones, board seat with governance rights, and a defined monitoring period

**Unexpected strategic development:**
- A third-party acquirer approaches the company with a premium bid
- The company announces a strategic review that is expected to result in a sale
- Elliott supports the process and exits into the takeout premium

**Thesis violation:**
- Underlying business deteriorates faster than expected
- New competition or regulatory risk not incorporated in original analysis
- Elliott exits the position at a loss and deploys capital elsewhere

## Campaign Execution Checklist

Before initiating any public campaign:

- [ ] **Entry window confirmed**: One of the four high-probability windows is present
- [ ] **Position size**: At least 5% established (13D threshold crossed)
- [ ] **Intrinsic value gap**: Minimum 30% discount to realistic intrinsic value at current price
- [ ] **Shareholder base modeled**: Achievable vote % exceeds 50% with ISS support
- [ ] **Governance analysis completed**: Specific, demonstrable governance failures documented (see 06-governance-analysis.md)
- [ ] **Director nominees identified**: 2-3 highly credentialed candidates vetted and committed
- [ ] **Private engagement completed**: At least one substantive written proposal delivered and rejected
- [ ] **ISS pre-engagement initiated**: Governance white paper drafted and preliminary ISS analyst contact made
- [ ] **Legal review**: 13D language reviewed; HSR status confirmed; settlement agreement template prepared
- [ ] **Campaign budget approved**: Proxy fight cost ($10-20M) pre-approved; duration assumes 12-18 month commitment

> "The most important moment in a campaign is before it starts — when you decide the conditions are right to move. Everything else is execution. Get the timing right and the rest follows." — Elliott Management
