# Debt Cycles: Short-Term and Long-Term

> When to read this file: when analyzing credit conditions, when evaluating whether a boom or bust is approaching, when assessing leverage in an economy or sector, or when studying historical debt crises.

> "All big economic crises are the result of too much debt." — Principles for Navigating Big Debt Crises

---

## The Short-Term Debt Cycle (5-8 Years)

### The Six Phases

**Phase 1: Early Expansion**
- Interest rates are low (recently cut by central bank)
- Credit begins to expand
- Spending picks up but from a low base
- Unemployment is still high but falling
- Investor sentiment: cautious optimism
- **Ideal for**: Risk assets — stocks, high-yield, cyclicals

**Phase 2: Mid-Expansion**
- Credit expansion accelerates
- Employment improves significantly
- Corporate profits grow
- Asset prices rise
- Central bank begins to consider tightening
- **Ideal for**: Equities, real estate, corporate credit

**Phase 3: Late Expansion**
- Credit growth is strong, perhaps excessive
- Full employment or near-full employment
- Inflation begins to rise
- Asset valuations are stretched
- Lending standards loosen ("covenant-lite" returns)
- **Warning**: Risk is building, even though everything feels good
- **Ideal for**: Reducing risk, building cash, commodities/inflation hedges

**Phase 4: Tightening / Peak**
- Central bank raises rates to combat inflation
- Credit becomes more expensive
- The weakest borrowers begin to struggle
- Asset prices plateau or begin to decline
- Sentiment shifts from confidence to uncertainty
- **Ideal for**: Cash, short-duration bonds, defensive equities

**Phase 5: Contraction / Recession**
- Credit contracts — borrowers can't or won't borrow, lenders can't or won't lend
- Spending falls → income falls → spending falls further
- Unemployment rises
- Asset prices decline, sometimes sharply
- Defaults increase
- Sentiment: fear, sometimes panic
- **Ideal for**: Long-duration government bonds, gold, distressed debt (late in the phase)

**Phase 6: Easing / Bottoming**
- Central bank cuts rates aggressively
- Government may implement fiscal stimulus
- Credit begins to stabilize
- The worst of the defaults are past
- Sentiment: despair, but conditions are improving
- **Ideal for**: Risk assets at distressed prices — the best buying opportunity
- **Transition**: Leads back to Phase 1

### Short-Term Cycle Indicators

| Indicator | Early | Mid | Late | Peak | Contraction | Bottom |
|-----------|-------|-----|------|------|-------------|--------|
| Interest rates | Low/falling | Stable/rising | Rising | High | Falling rapidly | Very low |
| Credit growth | Recovering | Strong | Excessive | Slowing | Negative | Bottoming |
| Unemployment | High, falling | Moderate, falling | Low | Very low | Rising | High |
| Inflation | Low | Moderate | Rising | High | Falling | Low |
| Sentiment | Cautious | Confident | Euphoric | Nervous | Fearful | Despair |
| Best action | Buy risk | Hold risk | Reduce risk | Sell risk | Hold safe assets | Buy risk |

## The Long-Term Debt Cycle (50-75 Years)

### The Macro Supercycle

> "The long-term debt cycle is the most important cycle. It determines the backdrop against which all short-term cycles play out." — Principles for Navigating Big Debt Crises

**The buildup (40-60 years):**
- Each short-term cycle ends with slightly more total debt than the previous one
- Interest rates trend downward over decades (each cut goes to a lower low)
- Financial innovation creates new forms of debt (securitization, derivatives)
- Leverage in the system gradually increases
- **This is sustainable as long as income growth keeps pace with debt service**

**The crisis point:**
- Total debt/GDP reaches levels where even low interest rates can't prevent strain
- Interest rates hit zero — the central bank's primary tool is exhausted
- A triggering event (financial crisis, pandemic, geopolitical shock) exposes the fragility
- The economy enters a **deleveraging** (see 05-deleveraging.md)

### Historical Long-Term Cycles

| Period | Buildup Phase | Crisis Point | Resolution |
|--------|--------------|--------------|------------|
| 1920s-1930s (US) | Roaring Twenties credit boom | 1929 crash, debt unsustainable | Depression, then New Deal + WWII spending |
| 1980s-1990s (Japan) | Asset bubble, extreme leverage | 1989 crash, debt/GDP >250% | Three "lost decades," ongoing |
| 2000s (US) | Housing bubble, securitization | 2008 financial crisis | QE + fiscal stimulus ("beautiful deleveraging") |
| 1920s (Germany) | War debt, reparations | Unsustainable sovereign debt | Hyperinflationary deleveraging |

### Where Are We Now in the Long-Term Cycle?

**Key metrics to assess:**

1. **Total debt/GDP**: US at ~350%, Japan at ~400%, China at ~300%
2. **Interest rates**: Near zero or negative in many developed economies (limited room to cut)
3. **Central bank balance sheets**: Dramatically expanded from QE programs
4. **Fiscal deficits**: Large and growing in most developed economies
5. **Wealth inequality**: High and rising (a late-cycle characteristic)

> "When I look at the indicators today, I see a world that looks a lot like the late stages of several past long-term debt cycles." — Principles for Navigating Big Debt Crises

## Credit as the Amplifier

### Why Credit Causes Cycles

If there were no credit — only cash transactions — economic growth would track productivity: steady, gradual, undramatic. Credit creates cycles because:

1. **Credit amplifies the upside**: Borrowing enables spending beyond current income, accelerating growth
2. **Creditworthiness is self-reinforcing**: Rising income and asset values make borrowers look safer, enabling more borrowing
3. **The virtuous cycle reverses**: When asset prices fall, borrowers look riskier, lenders pull back, spending falls, asset prices fall further

> "Credit creates a mechanical, predictable series of events. By understanding this, you can position yourself for what's likely to come." — Dalio

### Dalio's Credit Health Indicators

| Indicator | Healthy | Warning | Dangerous |
|-----------|---------|---------|-----------|
| Debt service/income (households) | <10% | 10-14% | >14% |
| Corporate debt/EBITDA | <3x | 3-5x | >5x |
| Credit growth vs GDP growth | ≈ equal | Credit growing 1.5x+ faster | Credit growing 2x+ faster |
| Lending standards | Strict | Easing | "Anything goes" |
| Asset prices/fundamentals | In line | 20%+ premium | Bubble territory |

## Practical Cycle Navigation

### Dalio's Rules for Different Cycle Positions

**Rule 1**: In early expansion → **increase risk**. Buy equities, credit, real assets. The recovery is real and underappreciated.

**Rule 2**: In mid-expansion → **maintain risk but diversify**. The easy money has been made. Focus on quality.

**Rule 3**: In late expansion → **reduce risk**. Build cash, increase quality, reduce leverage. The next contraction is coming.

**Rule 4**: At the peak → **be defensive**. The transition from expansion to contraction is sudden and violent.

**Rule 5**: In contraction → **be patient**. Don't try to catch the falling knife. Wait for evidence of policy response.

**Rule 6**: At the bottom → **be aggressive**. This is where fortunes are made. Buy quality assets at distressed prices.

> "The most important thing I've learned is that the time to invest is when things look worst, and the time to be cautious is when things look best." — Dalio
