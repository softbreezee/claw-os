# All Weather Portfolio Construction

> When to read this file: when building or evaluating a diversified portfolio, when applying risk parity principles, when understanding why traditional 60/40 portfolios are poorly diversified, or when discussing how to construct portfolios that perform in any economic environment.

The All Weather portfolio is Dalio's most influential contribution to practical investing. It emerged from a simple question: "What portfolio would I want if I didn't know what the future held?"

> "The biggest mistake investors make is to believe that what happened in the recent past is likely to persist. They project recent performance into the future, and then position their portfolios accordingly." — Principles

---

## The Problem with Traditional Portfolios

### Why 60/40 Is Not Diversified

Most investors believe a portfolio of 60% stocks and 40% bonds is "balanced." Dalio demonstrated that this is an illusion:

> "A 60/40 portfolio gets about 90% of its risk from equities. It's not a balanced portfolio — it's an equity portfolio with a bond kicker." — Bridgewater research

**The math:**
- Stocks are ~3-4x more volatile than bonds
- In a 60/40 portfolio, the equity portion contributes ~85-90% of total portfolio risk
- When stocks crash (2000-2002, 2008, 2020), the 40% bond allocation is insufficient to offset equity losses
- The portfolio is effectively making a single bet: that equities will do well

**What actually diversifies:**
- Diversification is about **risk balance**, not dollar balance
- True diversification means each part of the portfolio contributes roughly equally to total risk
- This requires allocating more dollars to lower-volatility assets (bonds) and fewer to higher-volatility assets (stocks)

## The Four Economic Environments

### Dalio's 2×2 Framework

Asset prices are driven by two factors: **growth** and **inflation**. Each can be rising or falling. This creates four environments:

| | Growth Rising | Growth Falling |
|---|--------------|----------------|
| **Inflation Rising** | **Reflation**: Commodities ↑, TIPS ↑, EM ↑, Stocks ↑ (moderate) | **Stagflation**: Gold ↑, Commodities ↑, Bonds ↓, Stocks ↓ |
| **Inflation Falling** | **Goldilocks**: Stocks ↑↑, Corp Bonds ↑, Commodities ↓ | **Deflation**: Long Treasuries ↑↑, Gold ↑, Stocks ↓, Commodities ↓ |

**Key insight:** We don't know which environment will prevail next. But we CAN identify assets that perform well in each environment and build a portfolio that is balanced across all four.

### Asset Class Behavior by Environment

| Asset Class | Goldilocks | Reflation | Deflation | Stagflation |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| Equities | ✅ Strong | ✅ Moderate | ❌ Weak | ❌ Weak |
| Long-term Treasuries | Neutral | ❌ Weak | ✅ Strong | Neutral |
| TIPS (inflation-linked) | Neutral | ✅ Strong | Neutral | ✅ Strong |
| Commodities | ❌ Weak | ✅ Strong | ❌ Weak | ✅ Moderate |
| Gold | ❌ Weak | Neutral | ✅ Moderate | ✅ Strong |
| Corporate Credit | ✅ Strong | ✅ Moderate | ❌ Weak | ❌ Weak |

## The All Weather Allocation

### Conceptual Framework

Allocate **equal risk** to each of the four environments:
- 25% of risk to "growth rises" assets
- 25% of risk to "growth falls" assets
- 25% of risk to "inflation rises" assets
- 25% of risk to "inflation falls" assets

**Because asset classes have different volatilities**, equal risk does NOT mean equal dollar allocation. Lower-volatility assets (bonds) get more dollars; higher-volatility assets (commodities) get fewer.

### Simplified All Weather Allocation

A practical approximation:

| Asset Class | Allocation | Role | Environment |
|-------------|-----------|------|-------------|
| Long-term Treasuries | 40% | Deflation hedge | Growth falls + Inflation falls |
| Stocks (broad market) | 30% | Growth exposure | Growth rises |
| Intermediate Treasuries | 15% | Stability, deflation | Growth falls |
| Gold | 7.5% | Inflation + crisis hedge | Inflation rises + Deflation |
| Commodities (broad) | 7.5% | Inflation hedge | Inflation rises |

> "This isn't about predicting which environment will prevail — it's about being prepared for any of them." — Dalio

### Why So Much in Bonds?

Stocks are 3-4x more volatile than bonds. To equalize risk contribution:
- If stocks have 15% annual volatility and bonds have 5%, you need 3x more dollars in bonds to get equal risk
- This is counterintuitive for investors trained to think in dollar terms
- But it's mathematically correct for risk balance

## Risk Parity: The Underlying Principle

### What Risk Parity Means

> "The idea is simple: instead of concentrating your portfolio's risk in one asset class (usually equities), spread the risk evenly across multiple, uncorrelated asset classes." — Bridgewater research

**Traditional approach**: Allocate 60% to stocks, 40% to bonds → 90% of risk from equities
**Risk parity approach**: Allocate to equalize risk contribution → each asset class contributes ~equally

### How to Implement Risk Parity

1. **Estimate the volatility** of each asset class
2. **Estimate the correlation** between asset classes
3. **Calculate the portfolio weights** that equalize each asset's contribution to total portfolio risk
4. **Use moderate leverage** (if allowed) to bring the total portfolio return up to the target level

**The leverage question:**
Risk parity often results in a portfolio with lower expected return than a stock-heavy portfolio (because it holds more low-return bonds). To compensate, Bridgewater uses modest leverage (1.5-2x) to amplify the balanced portfolio.

> "It's better to have a leveraged balanced portfolio than an unleveraged unbalanced one." — Dalio

**For individual investors:** The simplified All Weather allocation above does NOT require leverage and still provides meaningful diversification.

## Correlation: The Hidden Variable

### Why Correlations Matter More Than Volatilities

> "Most investors focus on individual asset returns and volatilities. What matters most is correlation — how assets move relative to each other." — Bridgewater research

**The key correlations:**
- **Stocks vs. Bonds**: Usually negative (bonds rise when stocks fall) — this is why 60/40 works... most of the time
- **Stocks vs. Commodities**: Low to moderate correlation — good for diversification
- **Bonds vs. Commodities**: Usually negative — they respond oppositely to inflation
- **Gold vs. Everything**: Low correlation to most assets — genuine diversifier

**The danger:** Correlations are NOT stable. In extreme environments (2008, 2020 March), correlations can spike — everything falls together. This is when "diversification fails" and only truly uncorrelated assets (high-quality government bonds, gold) provide protection.

## Practical Portfolio Assessment

When evaluating any portfolio through Dalio's lens:

1. **Risk contribution analysis**: What percentage of total portfolio risk comes from each asset class?
   - If >60% from equities → portfolio is equity-dependent (fragile in growth-falling environments)

2. **Environment stress test**: How would this portfolio perform in each of the four environments?
   - If it fails in 2+ environments → insufficient diversification

3. **Correlation check**: Are the diversifying assets truly uncorrelated, or do they share hidden exposures?
   - Example: EM stocks + commodities + high-yield bonds all correlate with risk appetite

4. **Inflation protection**: Does the portfolio have adequate protection against unexpected inflation?
   - TIPS, commodities, gold provide inflation protection
   - Long-duration nominal bonds are devastated by inflation

5. **Deflation protection**: Does the portfolio have adequate protection against deflation/depression?
   - Long-duration government bonds and cash provide deflation protection
   - Equities, commodities, and credit perform poorly in deflation
